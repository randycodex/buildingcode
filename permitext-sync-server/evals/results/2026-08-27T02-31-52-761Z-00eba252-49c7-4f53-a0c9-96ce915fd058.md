**PC 403.1 — canonical section 11909**

> The number of fixtures for building or nonaccessory tenant space used for assembly purposes by fewer than 75 persons and classified as Group B occupancy in accordance with Section 303.1, Exception 2 of the New York City Building Code shall be permitted to be calculated in accordance with the requirements for Assembly occupancies.

> To determine the occupant load of each sex, the total occupant load shall be divided in half. Fractional numbers resulting from applying the fixture ratios of Table 403.1 shall be rounded up to the next whole number. For calculations involving multiple occupancies, such fractional numbers for each occupancy shall first be summed and then rounded up to the next whole number. Fixture calculations in Group B office occupancies shall utilize the total occupant load on a given floor to determine the number of fixtures required for that floor.
> Exception: The total occupant load shall not be required to be divided in half where approved statistical data indicates a distribution of the sexes of other than 50 percent of each sex.

### Permitext answer

Not automatically. For an accessory assembly room with fewer than 75 occupants, BC 303.1.3 classifies the room as Group B (or part of the accessory occupancy) but expressly permits its plumbing-fixture count to be calculated under the Assembly-occupancy requirements. That is the direct fixture-calculation option for this room; the supplied evidence does not establish that the normal Group B fixture ratios may alternatively be used.

PC 403.1 generally directs fixture counts to Table 403.1 using the Building Code occupancy classification and occupant load. Its separately supplied Assembly permission is limited to a building or **nonaccessory tenant space** used for assembly by fewer than 75 persons, so it is not independent authority for this accessory multipurpose room. If calculating fixtures, apply the applicable Table 403.1 ratio to each sex's occupant load, ordinarily divided 50/50, and round fractions up; a different sex distribution requires approved statistical data.

A numerical fixture count cannot be determined from the supplied passages because Table 403.1's applicable Assembly row and ratios are not included, and the room's actual Assembly use category, occupant load, and whether shared/existing facilities are proposed to serve it remain unknown.

**What the selected evidence establishes**

- Accessory assembly-space fixture option: An assembly room or space with an occupant load below 75 that is accessory to another occupancy is classified as Group B or as part of that occupancy. Despite that classification, its plumbing fixtures are permitted to be calculated under the requirements for Assembly occupancies.
- Fixture counts follow Table 403.1: Fixtures must be provided for the occupancy type and in the minimum number shown in Table 403.1. Occupant load and occupancy classification are determined under the New York City Building Code.
- Separate Assembly permission is limited: The separately stated Plumbing Code permission to calculate under Assembly requirements applies to a building or nonaccessory tenant space used for assembly by fewer than 75 persons and classified as Group B under the identified Building Code provision. Its stated subject scope does not establish a separate permission for an accessory assembly room.
- Calculation method: For each fixture type, apply the applicable Table 403.1 ratio to each sex's occupant load, ordinarily one-half of total occupant load. Round fractional fixture results up. For multiple occupancies, sum the fractional results by occupancy before rounding. A distribution other than 50 percent for each sex may be used only where approved statistical data supports it.
