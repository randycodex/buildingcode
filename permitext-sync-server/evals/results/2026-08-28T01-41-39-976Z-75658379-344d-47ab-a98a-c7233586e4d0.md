### Permitext answer

No—not on the present record. The M-to-B change ordinarily triggers the new/amended Certificate of Occupancy requirements because it changes occupancy group and may be inconsistent with the last-issued Certificate of Occupancy. AC 28-118.3 provides a narrow exception: it says those two Certificate-of-Occupancy provisions are not to be interpreted to require a new or amended Certificate of Occupancy for an M-to-B (or B-to-M) change only if **all** six listed criteria are met.

The stated 70- and 72-person loads are each below 74, and the described ground-floor exterior public access, unchanged required exits, and unchanged Certificate-of-Occupancy live load correspond to four of those criteria. But these are applicant representations, not established compliance facts; moreover, the required accessibility and fire-protection criterion has not been documented. Therefore the applicant cannot presently rely on the exception.

In particular, the Group M-to-Group B change triggers the supplied fire-protection rule throughout the changed space. If it changes the building’s main use or dominant occupancy, the fire-protection requirement instead extends to the entire building as if newly erected. For accessibility, if the building is confirmed to be a prior-code building, Chapter 11 applies to its alteration/change of use or occupancy; the occupancy-classification change requires accessible features and construction throughout the space, including its immediate entrance(s). The specific measures required by Chapters 9 and 11 are not established by the passages supplied.

To establish eligibility for the exception, provide: (1) the last-issued Certificate of Occupancy (or applicable Certificate of Completion) and approved records, including the stated live load and existing authorized use; (2) substantiation that the alteration is within the same zoning use group—the NYC Zoning Resolution was not available in this research; (3) occupant-load calculations under the applicable table showing both conditions do not exceed 74 persons; (4) drawings or other records substantiating ground-floor location, direct exterior public access, and no required-exit change; (5) structural/use documentation showing no change to the live load stated on the existing Certificate of Occupancy; (6) documentation of the applicable accessibility scope and resulting compliance, including confirmation that this is a prior-code building; (7) the Chapter 9 fire-protection scope and documentation that the changed space complies, plus confirmation whether the change affects the building’s main use or dominant occupancy; and (8) confirmation that all other requirements applicable to the alteration are satisfied. The exception only addresses whether AC 28-118.3.1 and 28-118.3.2 require a new or amended Certificate of Occupancy; it does not itself establish compliance with the underlying alteration requirements.

**What the selected evidence establishes**

- Ordinary Certificate-of-Occupancy rule and limited exception: For completed buildings or open lots, AC 28-118.3.1 through 28-118.3.4 apply. Ordinarily, a change from one occupancy group to another cannot be occupied or used until the commissioner issues a Certificate of Occupancy for completed permitted work consistent with approved construction documents and applicable law. A change inconsistent with the last Certificate of Occupancy or applicable Certificate of Completion likewise requires a new or amended Certificate of Occupancy. But AC 28-118.3 states that these two provisions are not interpreted to require a new or amended Certificate of Occupancy for a mercantile-to-business or business-to-mercantile change if every listed criterion is met.
- Six cumulative eligibility criteria: The exception requires that the alteration remain within the same zoning use group; that the maximum occupant load of the individual establishment, both existing and proposed, not exceed 74 persons under the applicable specified occupant-load table; that the establishment be on the ground floor and publicly accessed directly from the exterior; that it comply, or be made to comply, with all other applicable alteration requirements including accessibility and the cited fire-protection requirements; that it not require a change in required exits, except that relocation of same-size-or-larger exit doors is not a change in required exits; and that it not require a change from the live load stated on the existing Certificate of Occupancy.
