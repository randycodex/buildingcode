## product-example-ramp-2022 — turn 1

Question: what are the requirements for designing a ramp?

Status: completed; deterministic review: PASS

For a typical NYC ramp that is part of a means of egress or an accessible route, use a **maximum 1:12 running slope**, **maximum 1:48 cross slope**, **30 inches maximum rise per run**, **36 inches minimum clear width**, required landings, handrails when the rise exceeds 6 inches, slip-resistant securely attached surfacing, and edge protection unless an express exception applies. These are baseline rules in the current 2022 NYC Building Code.

| Item | Requirement | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | BC 1012 applies to ramps used as a component of a means of egress. Curb ramps must comply with ICC A117.1. Except for specified accessible-route aisles, ramped aisles in assembly rooms/spaces follow BC § 1029. Vehicle ramps in parking garages used for pedestrian exit access are not required to comply with BC §§ 1012.3–1012.10 when they are not an accessible route serving accessible parking or other required accessible elements and are not part of an accessible means of egress. | BC § 1012.1 |
| Running slope | Means-of-egress and accessible-route ramps: no steeper than 1:12 (stated as 8-percent slope). Other pedestrian ramps: no steeper than 1:8 (12.5 percent). Group A aisle ramps follow BC § 1029.13. A ramp may be as steep as 1:7 (14 percent) only when it serves as part of a **nonaccessible** means of egress in a garage no more than three stories high and serving no more than one level below grade. | BC § 1012.2 |
| Cross slope | No steeper than 1:48 (2 percent). | BC § 1012.3 |
| Rise | Each ramp run: 30 inches maximum vertical rise. | BC § 1012.4 |
| Width | At least 36 inches clear between handrails, if provided, or other permissible projections. A means-of-egress ramp must also meet the corridor width and capacity required by BC § 1020.2. | BC § 1012.5.1 |
| Landings | Provide landings at the top and bottom of every ramp, turns, entrances, exits, and doors. Landings may not slope more than 1:48 in any direction and may not have changes in level. Landing width must equal or exceed the widest adjoining run. | BC §§ 1012.6, 1012.6.1–.2 |
| Landing size | Landing length is normally 60 inches minimum; a change-of-direction landing between ramp runs is normally 60 by 60 inches minimum. In an individual Group R-2 or R-3 dwelling/sleeping unit not required to be an Accessible, Type B+NYC, or Type B unit, the respective minimums may be 36 inches and 36 by 36 inches. Where a ramp is not part of an accessible route, its landing need not be more than 48 inches long in the direction of travel. | BC §§ 1012.6.3–.4 |
| Doors at landings | ICC A117.1-required door maneuvering clearances may overlap the required landing area. | BC § 1012.6.5 |
| Handrails | Handrails are required on both sides where ramp rise exceeds 6 inches. Ramped aisles are excepted only where BC § 1029.15 permits; handrails must comply with BC § 1014. | BC § 1012.8 |
| Edge protection | Provide edge protection on each side of ramp runs and on landing sides not adjoining another ramp run or stairway. A solid barrier must extend at least 4 inches above the floor or ground surface; another barrier type must prevent passage of a 4-inch-diameter sphere where any part of the sphere is within 4 inches of that surface. Edge protection is not required: (1) on a ramp not required to have handrails **if** it has flared sides complying with ICC A117.1 curb-ramp provisions; (2) at a landing side where the vertical drop-off is no more than 1/2 inch within 10 inches horizontally of the required landing area; or (3) where the ramp-run or landing surface extends at least 12 inches beyond the inside face of a BC § 1014-compliant handrail. | BC §§ 1012.10, 1012.10.1 |
| Surface / accessibility | Ramp surfaces must be slip-resistant and securely attached. Buildings and facilities must be designed and constructed to be accessible in accordance with ICC A117.1 and the NYC code. | BC §§ 1012.7.1, 1101.2 |

**Design implication:** At the 1:12 maximum slope, a 30-inch rise requires at least **30 feet (360 inches) of horizontal run**, followed by a landing before another run. This does not establish the total ramp configuration or the corridor-based egress width.

The applicable requirements depend on the ramp’s function—such as means of egress, accessible route, curb ramp, assembly aisle, or parking-garage pedestrian exit. The supplied evidence incorporates ICC A117.1 and cross-references BC §§ 1014, 1020.2, 1029.13, and 1029.15, but does not include their detailed text.

## product-example-corridor-accessibility-2022 — turn 1

Question: What's the minimum hall towards a fire escape that does not require ADA?

Status: completed; deterministic review: PASS

There is **no special minimum hall width that becomes automatically non-ADA because it leads to a fire escape** in the supplied 2022 NYC code text. The applicable Table 1020.2 minimum depends on the hall’s use and occupant load—not its direction toward a fire escape.

| Hall/corridor condition | Minimum width | Authority |
|---|---:|---|
| Any facilities not listed below | **44 in.** | BC §1020.2 |
| Occupant load of 50 or less | **36 in.**, except as otherwise required by Chapter 11” | BC §1020.2 |
| Within a dwelling unit, Group I-1 or R-1 | **36 in.**, except as otherwise required by Section 1107” | BC §1020.2 |
| Within a dwelling unit, Group R-2 or R-3 | **30 in.**, except as otherwise required by Section 1107” | BC §1020.2 |
| Access to mechanical, plumbing, or electrical systems/equipment | **24 in.** | BC §1020.2 |

So, on the supplied evidence, **44 inches is the general Table 1020.2 baseline**; a 36-inch or 30-inch width is available only if the hall fits the stated row and qualifier. The table does not establish a separate reduced width merely because the hall leads to a fire escape. It also does not establish that a qualifying reduced-width hall is outside Chapter 11 accessibility.

Where a building or portion is required to be accessible, at least one accessible route must be provided to each portion, accessible entrances, accessible pedestrian walkways, and the public way. The stated exceptions are: stories or mezzanines exempted under §1104.4; specified fixed-seating assembly levels without wheelchair spaces; vertical access to qualifying elevated courtroom employee workstations; and recreational facilities only to the extent specified by §1110. (BC §1104.3.) Buildings and facilities must be designed and constructed to be accessible under ICC A117.1 and the code; the supplied §1103.2 text says Chapter 11 exemptions apply to the extent specified in this section.” (BC §§1101.2, 1103.2.)

Noncontrolling federal guidance supplied with the question similarly states that there is no ADA hallway-width threshold below which a hall toward a fire escape is automatically outside ADA requirements.

Citations: BC 1020.2, BC 1104.3, BC 1103.2, BC 1101.2

Official supporting sources: https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds

## product-example-omh-bathroom-boundary — turn 1

Question: This feasibility must comply with NYS Office of Mental Health guidelines. Research the minimum bathroom, toilet, and ADA requirements.

Status: completed; deterministic review: REVIEW REQUIRED

**The supplied evidence does not establish NYS Office of Mental Health (OMH) bathroom, toilet, or ADA requirements.** No OMH program type, guideline, licensure rule, funding condition, or approval direction is supplied. The enacted 2022 NYC code does provide conditional baseline fixture, separation, ventilation, and limited unit-accessibility rules.

For fixture counts, the Building Code determines the occupancy classification and occupant load; Table 403.1 then supplies the minimums (PC §403.1). Potentially relevant table baselines are: Group B—water closets per sex: 1 for 1–20 persons, 2 for 21–45, 3 for 46–70, 4 for 71–100, 5 for 101–140, 6 for 141–190, then 1 additional per 50; lavatories per sex: 1 for 1–25, 2 for 26–50, 3 for 51–75, 4 for 76–115, 5 for 116–160, then 1 additional per 60; plus 1 drinking fountain per 100 and 1 service sink. I-1 residential care requires water closets per sex at 1 per 10, lavatories at 1 per 8, 1 bathtub/shower per 100, and 1 service sink. I-2 patient/ambulatory nursing-home care recipients require 1 water closet per room, 1 lavatory per room, 1 bathtub/shower per 15, 1 drinking fountain per 100, and 1 service sink per floor; its employee and visitor rows use different ratios. I-4 adult/child day care requires water closets per sex at 1 per 15, lavatories at 1 per 15, 1 bathtub/shower per 15, 1 drinking fountain per 100, and 1 service sink. Any fraction requires an additional fixture (PC §403.1).

Absent approved statistical data supporting another distribution, divide occupant load equally by sex, apply the relevant fixture ratios, and round up; for multiple occupancies, sum fractional results by occupancy before rounding up. Group B office calculations use the total occupant load on each floor (PC §403.1.1). Separate sex facilities are required where fixtures are required. Exceptions include dwelling/sleeping units and, only in structures or tenant spaces with facilities provided under PC §403.3, combined employee/public facilities for 30 or fewer total employees, customers, patrons, and visitors; employee-only facilities for 30 or fewer employees; or public-only facilities for 30 or fewer customers, patrons, and visitors (PC §403.2).

For ventilation, public toilet rooms require mechanical exhaust with no recirculation: 50 cfm per water closet or urinal if continuous, or 70 cfm if intermittent. The table's 20/50 cfm per-room rate applies specifically to toilet rooms and bathrooms in private dwellings; private hotel/motel/resort/dormitory bathrooms use 25/50 cfm per room. These per-room lower rates are allowed only when continuous while the served dwelling or sleeping unit is occupied; otherwise the higher intermittent rate applies (MC Table 403.3.1.1).

If a dwelling or sleeping unit is a Type B+NYC unit, each toilet room must have at least one compliant lavatory and water closet; each bathing room must have at least one compliant lavatory, water closet, and bathtub or shower, with fixtures arranged so travel between them remains within the room (BC §1107.2.2). A Type A toilet-and-bathing-room exception can limit the other rooms in that unit to specified accessible-route, walking-surface, doorway, operable-part, and reinforcement provisions, but only if one room meets the stated Type A conditions, including compliant fixtures in one area, compliant dispensers, and a medicine-cabinet shelf no higher than 44 inches if provided; it also requires the specified vertical grab bar and applies the stated doorway exception (BC §1107.2.2).

Separately, ADA.gov official guidance—noncontrolling here—states that the 2010 ADA Standards address toilet and bathing facilities but do not themselves establish OMH-specific facility requirements. It identifies water-closet clearance, seat/grab-bar, lavatory, and mobility-feature patient/resident room criteria. Provide the exact OMH program/facility type and applicable OMH design, licensure, funding, or approval standard before treating any OMH requirement as established.

## product-example-habitable-space-2022 — turn 1

Question: in the building code, where are the minimum sq ft, or minimum distance between wall for habitable spaces like a bedroom

Status: completed; deterministic review: PASS

For the **2022 NYC Building Code**, look at **BC § 1208.1** for minimum room width/plan dimension and **BC § 1208.3.1** for minimum net floor area.

| Item | Requirement | Authority |
|---|---:|---|
| Typical habitable room, including a bedroom in the defined dwelling-unit context | **80 sq. ft. net floor area** | BC § 1208.3.1 |
| Typical habitable space other than a kitchen | **8 ft minimum in every plan dimension** | BC § 1208.1 |
| Qualifying room open to an adjoining room | **70 sq. ft. net** and **7 ft minimum in every plan dimension**, if it has required natural light and natural ventilation plus an unobstructed opening of at least **60 sq. ft.** into the immediately adjoining room | BC §§ 1208.1, 1208.3.1 |
| Bedrooms in a dwelling unit with 3 or more bedrooms | Up to one-half of the bedrooms may be **7 ft minimum in every plan dimension** | BC § 1208.1 |
| Room in a Group R-1 dwelling unit | **60 sq. ft. net floor area** | BC § 1208.3.1 |
| Room in a Group R-1 dwelling or sleeping unit | **6 ft minimum in every plan dimension** | BC § 1208.1 |

So, for an ordinary bedroom subject to the baseline provisions, it needs both **80 net sq. ft.** and **8 ft in every plan dimension**. An 8 ft × 10 ft room meets those two numeric baseline values. A 7-ft-wide bedroom needs to qualify for one of the stated width exceptions; area alone does not create that exception. Bedrooms are expressly included in the definition of habitable space for dwelling units in Group R or I-1. (BC § 202)

These supplied provisions address area and plan dimensions only; they do not establish every separate condition for a room to qualify as a lawful bedroom, such as light, ventilation, egress, ceiling height, or existing-building provisions.

Citations: BC 1208.3.1, BC 1208.1, BC 202

Yes. The cited text is **BC § 715.4.7.1, Size limitations, in the 2014 NYC Building Code** contained in the supplied 2014 NYC Construction Codes archive. The prior answer’s 0.694-square-foot figure is only a conversion of 100 square inches; the enacted text states **100 square inches (0.065 m²)**. (BC § 715.4.7.1)

Correction to the earlier overgeneralization: the provision does **not** send every other fire-door glazing condition to NFPA 80. For fire doors located in fire walls, fire-protection-rated glazing is prohibited, except for a self-closing swinging door serving in a horizontal exit; that exception permits a vision panel no larger than 100 square inches and no dimension over 10 inches. For a 1½-hour fire door intended for a fire barrier, glazing is prohibited unless its area is no more than 100 square inches. The general rule for fire-protection-rated glazing used in fire doors is compliance with NFPA 80 size limitations. (BC § 715.4.7.1)
