The owner’s claim is not supported by the selected Building Code passages. If the café is beyond the building line, it must comply not only with BC 3111 but also with the Zoning Resolution, applicable Department of Consumer and Worker Protection and Department of Transportation commissioner requirements, and Chapter 32 projection limits. The cited text does not establish that an outdoor-dining authorization itself satisfies those requirements.

Sidewalk-café awnings, enclosures, fixtures, equipment, and removable platforms may not obstruct a building exit or a cellar access hatch or areaway. A platform may cover a cellar entrance or areaway only under the narrow stated exception: special application, commissioner permission, an easily removable and prominently designated platform designed under BC 3111.5, and an entrance or areaway not used as a required means of egress. Because the geometry, furniture locations, and cellar-access egress status are unknown, compliance cannot be determined.

Accessibility review is not eliminated. Sidewalk cafés and access to them must comply with Chapter 11. Where dining surfaces are provided, at least 10 percent of the total seating and standing spaces of each type of dining surface—but never fewer than one—must be accessible, distributed throughout the facility, and on a level accessed by an accessible route. The selected passages do not authorize a separate exterior count merely because outdoor dining is authorized.

For the limited question whether a place-of-assembly certificate of operation is required, seating in an enclosed sidewalk café must be added to interior seating unless it is separated by fire partitions complying with Section 713. Whether that rule applies remains unresolved because the record does not establish whether the café is enclosed or whether a qualifying fire partition is present. The selected text supplies no general authorization to count exterior seating separately for accessibility or other purposes.
