### Permitext answer

Not on the stated basis. The draft-curtain provision is Exception 4—not Exception 2—to BC 1019.3. Exception 2 permits an unenclosed exit-access stair only where the building is sprinklered throughout under BC 903.3.1.1; the stair serves only between the first and second stories above grade; both stories have the required number of approved independent exits under BC 1006, and never fewer than two means of egress; the interconnected stories are not open to other stories; and the unenclosed stair is remotely located under BC 1007. A draft curtain does not substitute for any of those Exception 2 conditions.

If the proposal instead relies on Exception 4, an open exit-access stair may be permitted only if the building is sprinklered throughout in accordance with BC 903.3.1.1; the vertical-opening area is no more than twice the stair’s horizontal projected area; and the opening has both a draft curtain and closely spaced sprinklers complying with NFPA 13 as modified by Appendix Q. In occupancies other than Groups B and M, the opening cannot connect more than four stories. The general rule remains that exit-access stairs in the scope of BC 1019.3 must be shaft-enclosed.

The supplied material does not include the drawing or identify the occupancy, sprinkler-system compliance, stories connected, opening-area comparison, or—as applicable—the independent-exit, story-openness, and remoteness conditions. Therefore, it cannot establish that the stair as shown qualifies under either exception.

**What the selected evidence establishes**

- Baseline enclosure requirement: For occupancies other than Groups H, I-2 and I-3, exit access stairways and ramps must be enclosed with a shaft enclosure complying with Section 713, unless an applicable exception permits otherwise.
