The owner’s claim cannot be supported from these passages. An outdoor-dining authorization does not eliminate the Building Code requirements identified here. If the café is beyond the building line, it must comply with Section 3111, the Zoning Resolution, applicable Department of Consumer and Worker Protection and Department of Transportation commissioner requirements, and Chapter 32 projection limits.

Café components—including furniture or equipment if they are fixtures or equipment of the café—cannot obstruct a building exit or a cellar access hatch or areaway. The limited exception allows only a commissioner-permitted, easily removable and prominently designated platform designed under Section 3111.5, upon special application, over a cellar entrance or areaway that is not a required means of egress. The supplied passages give no dimensions, so the stated proximity to the exit and hatch does not establish compliance.

Accessibility review is not eliminated. Sidewalk cafés and their access must comply with Chapter 11. In dining and drinking areas, interior and exterior floor areas must be accessible and on an accessible route, subject to the listed exceptions. Where dining surfaces are provided, at least 10 percent of the seating and standing spaces of each dining-surface type, and at least one, must be accessible, distributed throughout the facility, and on a level accessed by an accessible route.

Separate counting for the stated place-of-assembly certificate-of-operation determination is not established merely by outdoor authorization. For an enclosed sidewalk café, its seats must be added to indoor seats unless the café is separated from indoor seating by fire partitions complying with Section 713. If the café is not enclosed, the supplied passages do not state the counting rule.
