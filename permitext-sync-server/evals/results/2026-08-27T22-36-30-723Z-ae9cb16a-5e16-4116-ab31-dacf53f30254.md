The applicant’s eligibility for the Certificate-of-Occupancy exception is **not established on the present record**. The ordinary rule requires a Certificate of Occupancy for an alteration changing an occupancy group, and a new or amended Certificate of Occupancy for a change inconsistent with the last Certificate of Occupancy. But, for a completed building, the mercantile-to-business exception means those two provisions are not interpreted to require a new or amended Certificate of Occupancy if—and only if—all six stated criteria are met.

Several stated facts align with the exception if they are correct: 70 and 72 are each below 74; the establishment is stated to be on the ground floor with direct public exterior access; and the work is stated not to require changes to required exits or to the live load stated on the existing Certificate of Occupancy. The 74-person criterion nevertheless requires the applicable occupant-load calculations, and the exception also requires that the change remain within the same zoning use group.

Most importantly, lack of documented accessibility and fire-protection compliance does not itself establish noncompliance or create a separate documentation condition in the cited exception. It does mean the record does not establish satisfaction of criterion 4: the altered establishment must comply, or be made to comply, with all other applicable alteration requirements, including accessibility and fire-protection requirements. For this Group M-to-Group B change, Chapter 9 fire-protection systems governed by that chapter must be provided throughout the changed space. If the change is to the building’s main use or dominant occupancy, the required scope is the entire building as if newly erected. Chapter 11 accessible features and construction governed by that chapter must be provided throughout the changed space, including its immediate entrance(s), because the alteration changes the occupancy classification.

Accordingly, the applicant may rely on the exception only if the six cumulative conditions are actually met. What remains to be established is the same-zoning-use-group determination; applicable existing and proposed occupant-load calculations; the existing Certificate of Occupancy and its stated live load; whether the alteration requires a change in required exits; compliance, or work that will achieve compliance, with the applicable accessibility and fire-protection requirements; whether the change affects the building’s main use or dominant occupancy; and any other requirements applicable to the alteration.
