### Permitext answer

Yes. For multiple occupancies, apply the applicable Table 403.1 ratio to each occupancy and fixture type, using the occupant load for each sex by default, then add the fractional results and round the combined result up to the next whole number. The default sex calculation divides total occupant load in half; another distribution may be used only where approved statistical data supports it. For a Group B office occupancy, however, the calculation must use the total occupant load on the given floor.

The multipurpose room may use Assembly fixture requirements if it is actually used for assembly purposes, has an occupant load below 75, and is accessory to another occupancy. It remains classified as Group B or as part of the other occupancy, but its fixture count may be calculated under Assembly requirements. PC 403.1 also provides a separately limited Assembly-calculation permission for building or nonaccessory tenant space under 75 occupants that is classified as Group B; that separate permission does not replace the direct accessory-room rule.

A final fixture count cannot be determined from the supplied facts. The applicable Table 403.1 rows and ratios, actual room functions and classifications, occupant loads, applicable Assembly category, accessory status of the multipurpose room, and any proposed use of existing or shared facilities remain unresolved.

**What the selected evidence establishes**

- Multiple-occupancy fractions are added before rounding: For each fixture type, apply the applicable Table 403.1 ratio to each occupancy. For multiple occupancies, sum the fractional results for each occupancy first and then round the combined result up to the next whole number. By default, determine each sex's occupant load by dividing the total occupant load in half; a different distribution is allowed only where approved statistical data indicates a distribution other than 50 percent for each sex. For Group B office occupancies, use the total occupant load on the given floor to determine the fixtures required for that floor.
- Fixture ratios follow occupancy and occupant load: Plumbing fixtures must be provided by occupancy type and in at least the numbers in Table 403.1. Occupant load and occupancy classification are determined under the New York City Building Code; an occupancy type not shown in Table 403.1 is considered individually by the commissioner.
- Qualifying accessory assembly room may use Assembly fixture requirements: A room or space used for assembly purposes, with fewer than 75 occupants, and accessory to another occupancy is classified as Group B or as part of that other occupancy. Its plumbing-fixture count is nevertheless permitted to be calculated under Assembly-occupancy requirements.
- Separate permission has a limited scope: A building or nonaccessory tenant space used for assembly purposes by fewer than 75 persons and classified as Group B under the specified Building Code provision is also permitted to use Assembly fixture requirements. This is a separate, expressly limited permission and does not displace the direct rule for an accessory assembly room.
