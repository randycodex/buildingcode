### Permitext answer

Not on door spacing alone. Under the 2022 NYC Building Code, stairs sharing a scissor-stair assembly ordinarily count as **one exit stairway**. For your Group R-2 building, they can be treated as separate exit stairways only under the stated R-2 exception.

Your 15-foot separation meets the exception’s minimum door-spacing condition (the doors must be at least 15 feet apart). But the exception also requires both: (1) stair-enclosure walls with at least a 2-hour fire-resistance rating, constructed of masonry or masonry equivalent under Department rules; and (2) the construction separating the two stairs to have the same minimum 2-hour rating and masonry-or-equivalent construction. If those conditions are documented, the shared/scissor arrangement is permitted under the R-2 exception; if not, count it as one exit stairway.

The supplied evidence is limited to the 2022 NYC Construction Codes passages provided here. It does not establish the ratings or materials of your enclosure and separating construction, or supply the applicable Department rules defining masonry equivalent.

**What the selected evidence establishes**

- Default scissor-stair counting rule: Stairs that share a scissor-stair assembly, common wall, floor, ceiling, or other enclosure must be counted as one exit stairway unless an applicable exception applies.
- Group R-2 exception and its cumulative conditions: For Group R-2 occupancies, stairs may share a scissor-stair assembly or other enclosure when the stair-enclosure walls are at least 2-hour fire-resistance-rated and masonry or masonry equivalent under Department rules, the doors are at least 15 feet apart, and the construction separating the stairs is also at least 2-hour fire-resistance-rated and masonry or masonry equivalent under Department rules. The stated 15-foot door separation satisfies only the spacing condition.
