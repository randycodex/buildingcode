### Permitext answer

No. The fact that storage was once lawful does not itself create an automatic right to revive it after the building was changed to office occupancy. The existing-building rule permits continuation of a lawful existing use or occupancy, but the re-establishment rule separately governs a prior use after a change has occurred.

If the former storage occupancy would not be lawful in a new building of the same construction class, it may not be re-established unless and until all applicable Code provisions and other applicable laws and rules for that re-established storage use have been complied with. Separately, changes in use or occupancy made after July 1, 2008 must comply with the Code, except as otherwise provided in the listed cross-referenced provisions; a noncomplying change is prohibited and is a violation.

The owner also cannot assume that no certificate action is needed. If storage would be inconsistent with the last-issued certificate of occupancy (or, where applicable, certificate of completion), a new or amended certificate of occupancy must be issued before the change. If alteration work changes an occupancy group or zoning use group, the space cannot be occupied or used until the commissioner issues a certificate of occupancy confirming that the permitted work was substantially completed in accordance with approved construction documents, the Code, and other applicable laws and rules.

First establish the authorized terms of the last-issued certificate and the approved documents; the former and proposed occupancy-group classifications and zoning use groups; whether storage would be lawful in a new building of the same construction class; the applicable requirements for the particular proposed storage activity; and the scope of any proposed or required alteration work. The owner's representation that the pre-2008 storage use was lawful should also be verified from records. Even if verified, that prior lawfulness alone does not establish automatic resumption.

**What the selected evidence establishes**

- Existing-use continuation is not automatic revival: A lawful use or occupancy of an existing building may continue unless a retroactive change is specifically required by the Code or other applicable laws or rules. That rule addresses continuation of a lawful existing use or occupancy; the separately supplied re-establishment rule applies after a change in use or occupancy has occurred.
- Re-establishing a prior use may require full compliance: Except as otherwise provided in the listed cross-referenced sections, a change in use or occupancy made after July 1, 2008 must comply with the Code. A change not in compliance is prohibited and is a violation. After a change has occurred, a prior use or occupancy that would not be lawful in a new building of the same construction class is prohibited from re-establishment unless and until all applicable Code provisions and other applicable laws and rules for that re-established use or occupancy have been complied with.
- A change inconsistent with the last certificate requires certificate action: No change inconsistent with the last-issued certificate of occupancy, or where applicable the last-issued certificate of completion, may be made unless and until the commissioner issues a new or amended certificate of occupancy. The provision also applies to a change that would bring the building, lot, or portion under a special provision of the Code or other applicable laws or rules.
- Certain altered occupancy or use changes require a certificate before use: Where a building, open lot, or portion is altered so as to change from one occupancy group to another or from one zoning use group to another, it may not be occupied or used until the commissioner issues a certificate of occupancy. The certificate must certify substantial completion of the permitted alteration work in accordance with approved construction documents, the Code, and other applicable laws and rules for the new occupancy or use.
