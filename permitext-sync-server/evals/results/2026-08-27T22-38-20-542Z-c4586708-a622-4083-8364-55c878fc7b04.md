### Permitext answer

No—provided the room is actually accessory to the apartment-building occupancy and its intended table-and-chair use is the basis for the occupant-load calculation. For an area without fixed seating, the supplied 15-net factor for unconcentrated tables and chairs gives an occupant load of 60 persons (900 ÷ 15). Because that is fewer than 75 persons, BC 303.1.3 requires an assembly-purpose room accessory to another occupancy to be classified as Group B or as part of that occupancy; it does not require Group A-3 classification. The provision separately permits the room’s plumbing-fixture count to be calculated under the assembly-occupancy requirements.

This result is limited to the described normal table-and-chair use. The accessory relationship is a distinct code condition: resident-only use in an apartment building does not by itself establish that the room is legally accessory to that occupancy. If another intended function or layout produces an occupant load of 75 or more, the supplied evidence does not establish the classification outcome for that use.

**What the selected evidence establishes**

- Table-and-chair occupant-load calculation: For an area without fixed seating, occupant load is determined by dividing the floor area by the Table 1004.1.3 factor assigned to the space function. The supplied table entries identify unconcentrated tables and chairs and a 15-net factor. Applied to the stated 900 net square feet, that produces 60 occupants.
