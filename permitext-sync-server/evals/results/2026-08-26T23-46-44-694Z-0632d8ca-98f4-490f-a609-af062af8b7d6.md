No. The cited NYC Building Code section does not prove that HCR requires a vanity. In the forward-approach water-closet layout, it requires 66 inches of rear clearance and 48 inches of side clearance, and it says that a compliant lavatory is **permitted** on the rear wall if it is at least 18 inches from the water-closet centerline. That sentence authorizes a particular lavatory location; it neither mentions nor requires a vanity.

The broader provision matters: where toilet or bathing rooms are provided in a Type B+NYC dwelling or sleeping unit, it requires at least one lavatory and water closet in each covered toilet room to comply with the specified accessibility sections; covered bathing rooms likewise include a complying lavatory, water closet, and bathtub or shower. That is a conditional requirement for a complying **lavatory**, not an expressed requirement for a vanity. The cited forward-approach subsection does not make rear-wall lavatory placement mandatory.

Nothing supplied establishes an HCR rule, funding condition, or agency guidance. Therefore, the NYC code text cannot establish what HCR requires. The applicable HCR design standard, program requirement, regulation, or official guidance would be needed to answer whether HCR requires a vanity.
