# Decision facts and bounded correction — 2026-09-08

The prior live laundry and water-heater answers gave supported direct conclusions but placed prospective design details in `missingFacts`. A shared generation/verifier instruction now limits that field to facts that can change or determine the requested result. It applies to positive, negative and non-numeric questions, while retaining applicability and exception facts and inputs needed for a requested design or calculation.

The no-cost replay exposed an important cost consequence: after the recorded laundry draft and first verification usage, a full-answer rewrite for this small field correction could not be reserved under the existing $0.50 turn cap. The water-heater rewrite could fit. Neither the cap nor the pricing ceilings was changed.

The verifier can now identify zero-based indices of entirely unnecessary `missingFacts` entries. A candidate correction may remove only those entries, only after a failed verification whose issues are all `unnecessary_qualification`. Mixed substantive issues, malformed/duplicate/out-of-range indices, passing responses and missing indices cannot activate the edit. Entries containing material applicability or exception facts must not be selected. A mixed necessary/optional entry requires a normal revision rather than deletion.

After the candidate edit, Research reapplies declared-fact protection and its ordinary evidence gates, then performs a new full semantic verification. Acceptance is never inferred from the first verifier's suggested edit. A failed final check prevents saving/charging the answer. Zoning retains its existing repair-eligibility and attempt limits. This path changes neither the narrative nor the supported rules, citations, other answer fields, or model routing. Saved metadata distinguishes a fact correction from regenerated answer prose.

Local contracts cover the production parser, invalid and mixed-issue inputs, immutability, exact preservation of the other answer fields, and four HTTP accept/reject replays of the two recorded cases. The HTTP replays use the recorded token usage instead of tiny fabricated usage counts. Each reaches the final verifier with one draft call and two verification calls, with no second answer-generation call; verdicts in those replays are synthetic.
