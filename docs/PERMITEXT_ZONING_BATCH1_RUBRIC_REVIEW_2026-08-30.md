## Disposition 1 — source Case 2 / expanded case 22

Case ID: `zr-candidate-b1-r6a-uap-insufficient-affordable-area`

Question: An 8,000-square-foot R6A lot outside an MIH area proposes 31,000 square feet of residential floor area, including 6,200 square feet intended to be affordable. Is being below the table's 3.90 FAR enough?

Problem with the current key: it requires a one-for-one UAP FAR increment, a 30,200-square-foot project maximum, an 800-square-foot deficiency, and a 7,000-square-foot affordable-floor-area requirement. The selected provisions do not establish that rule. The independent grader specifically found that Permitext correctly refused to invent it.

Proposed source-bound expected conclusion:

> No—not on the stated facts and selected evidence alone. The proposal is 3.875 FAR. That is below the table's 3.90 numerical ceiling but above the 3.00 standard-residence ceiling by 7,000 square feet. The stated intent to make 6,200 square feet affordable does not establish calculated affordable floor area, UAP-site eligibility, or entitlement to the qualifying-affordable-housing column. Those conclusions require the missing enacted UAP eligibility/linkage provisions and qualifying project documents.

Proposed required concepts:

1. Calculate 31,000 / 8,000 = 3.875 FAR, the 24,000-square-foot standard maximum, and the 31,200-square-foot table ceiling.
2. Distinguish a numerical ceiling from entitlement to use the qualifying-affordable-housing column.
3. State that owner-designated unit area does not by itself establish affordable floor area under the selected definition.
4. Identify the missing UAP eligibility/FAR-linkage provision and applicable affordable-housing agreement or restrictive declaration.
5. Do not invent the one-for-one increment, 30,200-square-foot maximum, or 800-square-foot deficiency unless added enacted evidence proves those rules.

Recommended disposition: **approve the proposed replacement key for evaluation testing only**.

## Disposition 2 — source Case 4 / expanded case 23

Case ID: `zr-candidate-b1-deep-through-lot-vertical-yard`

Question: A 200-foot-deep R7A through lot has residential wings facing both streets, a 30-foot open area between them, and 100-foot-tall wings. Is the open area enough for the rear yard equivalent?

Problem with the current key: it requires a general 190-foot threshold and separate 40-foot/60-foot height tiers. The selected R7A-specific ZR 24-382 provision instead governs a through lot at least 180 feet deep and requires a single 60-foot midblock open area. The independent grader found Permitext's use of the R7A-specific rule correct.

Proposed source-bound expected conclusion:

> No, if the stated 30 feet is the required depth measured perpendicular to the street lines. For the stated 200-foot-deep R7A through lot, ZR 24-382 requires a single open area at least 60 feet deep, midway or within five feet of midway between the street lines. A 30-foot depth is therefore 30 feet short. The plans must still confirm the dimension's orientation, midpoint location, qualifying lot geometry, applicable exceptions, and permitted-obstruction treatment.

Proposed required concepts:

1. Apply the R7A-specific at-least-180-foot rule and its 60-foot depth.
2. Calculate a 30-foot deficiency if the supplied 30-foot dimension is the regulated depth.
3. Require the midpoint or within-five-feet-of-midpoint location.
4. Distinguish a generic space between wings from a legally compliant rear yard equivalent.
5. Preserve uncertainty about dimensional orientation, geometry, exceptions, and obstructions.
6. Do not require the general 190-foot or height-tier analysis where the selected R7A-specific rule controls.

Recommended disposition: **approve the proposed replacement key for evaluation testing only**.
