# Permitext Research Benchmark — 40 Distinct NYC Code Research Cases

Purpose: test Permitext Research on a second set of materially different NYC code questions.

Benchmark basis:
- 2022 New York City Construction Codes, with later enacted amendments where applicable.
- 2025 New York City Energy Conservation Code for filings subject to the code beginning March 30, 2026.
- Current NYC existing-building transition rules: the new NYC Existing Building Code does not become effective until July 17, 2027.
- Each case intentionally distinguishes governing law, project facts, assumptions, exceptions, and missing evidence.
- The Ideal answer is a benchmark for substance and reasoning, not wording that Permitext must reproduce verbatim.

Evaluation principle: Permitext should stop at the boundary of the approved evidence. When a required definition, table, project fact, referenced standard, or outside authority is missing, identifying that limitation is part of the correct answer.

## Test 01

**Q:** A fire wall separating two portions of a building terminates at the underside of a combustible roof deck. Is that permitted, and what conditions could allow the fire wall to terminate without extending above the roof?

**Ideal answer:** The condition cannot be accepted merely because the fire wall reaches the roof deck. NYC BC §706.6 governs the vertical continuity of fire walls and generally requires the wall to extend from its supporting construction to the prescribed termination above the roof, subject to specific exceptions. Whether termination at or below the roof can be permitted depends on the roof construction, combustibility, fire-resistance of the roof assembly, protection of structural members, roof slope, protection for a prescribed distance on each side of the wall, and the exact exception being used. With a combustible roof deck, Permitext should be especially cautious: the selected exception must expressly permit that construction and every stated protection condition must be satisfied. The defensible conclusion from the stated facts is that termination at the underside of a combustible roof is not established as compliant; the applicable §706.6 exception and the roof assembly details are required.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §706.6 — Vertical continuity of fire walls.
- Applicable exceptions and subsections under BC §706.6.
- NYC BC Chapter 15 where roof construction or roof assembly classification is incorporated into the exception.

**Important qualifications:** The roof deck material, roof covering, structural framing, fire-resistance of the roof assembly, slope, parapet condition, and construction on both sides of the fire wall must be known. A fire wall is different from a fire barrier or fire partition and should not be analyzed under the wrong wall type.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- A fire wall may always stop at the roof deck.
- A combustible roof automatically satisfies a fire-wall termination exception.
- Fire-barrier or fire-partition continuity rules can substitute for the fire-wall provisions.
- The condition complies without identifying the exact §706.6 exception.

## Test 02

**Q:** An exterior wall is located 3 feet from an interior lot line. What fire-resistance rating is required, and are unprotected window openings permitted?

**Ideal answer:** Permitext should treat the wall rating and the opening limits as two separate questions. The required exterior-wall fire-resistance rating is determined from BC Table 602 using occupancy group, construction type, and fire separation distance. At a 3-foot fire separation distance, the applicable Table 602 row must be identified rather than assuming a universal 1-hour or 2-hour rating. Window openings are then evaluated separately under BC §705.8 and the applicable opening table, including whether openings are permitted, whether they must be protected, and the maximum percentage of wall area allowed at that distance. The answer therefore requires the occupancy, construction type, correctly measured fire separation distance, sprinkler status where the opening table recognizes it, and the amount of proposed opening area. Three feet from a lot line does not by itself establish either the wall rating or that windows are permitted.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC Table 602 — Fire-resistance rating requirements for exterior walls based on fire separation distance.
- NYC BC §705 — Exterior walls.
- NYC BC §705.8 and the applicable opening table.

**Important qualifications:** Fire separation distance is a defined Building Code measurement and may not always equal the designer's simple dimension to the lot line. Opening protection and allowable opening area must be checked independently from the wall rating.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Every wall 3 feet from a lot line is automatically a particular hourly rating.
- A rated exterior wall automatically permits windows.
- Opening-percentage limits can be ignored once the wall is rated.
- Fire separation distance can be measured from any convenient reference point.

## Test 03

**Q:** Does an exterior wall at the roof edge require a parapet, and under what conditions can the parapet be omitted?

**Ideal answer:** A parapet requirement must be evaluated from the specific exterior-wall and roof provisions rather than from building height alone. BC §705.11 regulates parapets at exterior walls and contains exceptions allowing omission where prescribed roof construction, fire-resistance, opening, and projection conditions are satisfied. If the wall is a fire wall, BC §706.6 creates a separate vertical-continuity analysis that can be more restrictive. Permitext should therefore first identify whether the wall is an ordinary exterior wall or a fire wall, then test every condition of the applicable parapet exception. A noncombustible roof or automatic sprinkler system by itself should not be treated as a universal parapet exemption.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §705.11 — Parapets at exterior walls.
- NYC BC §706.6 — Fire-wall vertical continuity where applicable.
- NYC BC Chapter 15 where roof construction is incorporated by the parapet exception.

**Important qualifications:** Construction type, roof deck and covering, wall rating, fire separation distance, projections, roof slope, and whether the wall is a fire wall are material facts.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Every roof requires a parapet.
- Sprinklers automatically eliminate parapet requirements.
- An exterior-wall parapet exception automatically applies to a fire wall.
- Combustible roof construction is irrelevant to the exception.

## Test 04

**Q:** An open stair connects two adjacent Group B office floors and is intended only for convenience, not as a required exit. Must the stair be enclosed?

**Ideal answer:** Not necessarily, but the stair is still a vertical opening and must satisfy a specific code allowance. Permitext should distinguish the Chapter 7 vertical-opening rules from the Chapter 10 exit-access-stair rules. A floor opening created by an unenclosed stair is generally regulated by BC §§712 and 713, and an exit access stairway is also subject to BC §1019. The code contains allowances for certain unenclosed two-story openings and exit access stairs, but those allowances have conditions involving the number of stories connected, occupancy, openness, floor-opening protection, sprinkler status where required, travel path, and other limitations. Calling the stair a "convenience stair" does not remove it from those provisions. The correct conclusion is conditional until Permitext identifies the precise allowance and verifies all of its conditions.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §712 — Vertical openings.
- NYC BC §713 — Shaft enclosures.
- NYC BC §1019 — Exit access stairways and ramps.
- The specific two-story-opening or exit-access-stair exception relied upon.

**Important qualifications:** Confirm whether the stair is part of any required means of egress, whether it connects exactly two stories, the occupancy on both levels, sprinkler status, floor-opening geometry, and any other vertical openings nearby.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- A convenience stair never requires enclosure.
- Every open stair is automatically an atrium.
- A stair that is not a required exit is exempt from Chapter 7.
- The stair complies simply because it connects only two floors.

## Test 05

**Q:** A four-story open volume connects a lobby with three upper floors. Can it be treated as an atrium instead of four separate shaft openings, and what major protections must be evaluated?

**Ideal answer:** Potentially, but only if the space satisfies the Building Code definition and the complete atrium provisions. BC §404 regulates atriums and addresses automatic sprinkler protection, smoke control, separation of the atrium from adjacent spaces, means of egress, interior finish, and other safeguards. BC §712 recognizes an atrium as a permitted vertical-opening condition when it complies with §404. Permitext should not classify a tall open space as an atrium merely because it looks or functions like one architecturally. The connected uses, number of stories, floor-opening geometry, smoke-control strategy, sprinkler system, separation construction, and egress configuration must be established. If §404 is not fully satisfied, the opening must be analyzed under another permitted vertical-opening method or enclosed as required.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §404 — Atriums.
- NYC BC §712 — Vertical openings.
- NYC BC §909 — Smoke control systems where required by §404.
- NYC BC Chapter 9 provisions referenced by the atrium requirements.

**Important qualifications:** An atrium is a regulated code condition, not a design label. Smoke control can require an engineered analysis and referenced standards beyond the selected paragraph.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Any multi-story lobby is an atrium.
- Sprinklers alone make an atrium compliant.
- Smoke-control requirements can be ignored if the atrium is open and visible.
- The atrium classification proves the entire vertical-opening design complies.

## Test 06

**Q:** Can a plumbing pipe penetrate a 2-hour shaft enclosure wall, and what protection is required at the penetration?

**Ideal answer:** A plumbing pipe can penetrate a rated shaft enclosure where the penetration is permitted and the fire-resistance of the assembly is maintained. BC §713 establishes shaft-enclosure requirements and BC §714 governs penetrations of fire-resistance-rated assemblies. Permitext should determine whether the condition is a through-penetration or membrane penetration, identify the pipe material and size, the wall assembly, annular space, insulation and other components, and then require a listed firestop system or another code-permitted method appropriate to that exact condition. The fact that the wall is 2-hour rated does not prohibit every penetration, but an unprotected penetration is not acceptable merely because the pipe is noncombustible.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §713 — Shaft enclosures.
- NYC BC §714 — Penetrations.
- The applicable listed firestop-system requirements incorporated by §714.

**Important qualifications:** Pipe material, diameter, annular space, wall construction, pipe insulation, and whether the penetration is through or membrane-only are needed to select the correct system.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- No piping may ever penetrate a shaft enclosure.
- Metal pipe needs no firestopping.
- Any generic fire caulk is sufficient.
- A firestop detail can be selected without matching the tested wall and penetrating item.

## Test 07

**Q:** A supply duct passes through a 1-hour fire barrier. Is a fire damper required, or can an exception permit the duct to pass without one?

**Ideal answer:** The duct should be evaluated under BC §717, which governs ducts and air-transfer openings in rated assemblies. A fire barrier normally creates an opening-protection issue, but §717 contains different rules and exceptions depending on the barrier's code function, duct construction, sprinkler protection, whether the duct penetrates one or multiple rated assemblies, and whether it is part of a smoke-control or other special system. Permitext must identify the exact wall type first: a fire barrier, fire partition, shaft enclosure, smoke barrier, and corridor wall can have different damper requirements. The correct answer is therefore not "all rated walls require dampers" or "sprinklers eliminate dampers"; it is whether the exact §717 provision or exception for this barrier and duct has been satisfied.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §717 — Ducts and air transfer openings.
- The applicable BC §717 subsection for fire barriers.
- NYC Mechanical Code duct construction provisions incorporated by the Building Code where applicable.

**Important qualifications:** The wall's code function, rating, duct size/material, sprinkler status, smoke-control role, and any shaft condition must be known.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Every rated-wall duct penetration requires a fire damper.
- Sprinklers remove all damper requirements.
- Fire dampers and smoke dampers are interchangeable.
- The answer can be determined without identifying the rated assembly type.

## Test 08

**Q:** A major interior alteration is proposed in an existing Group R-2 building. Does the work automatically require sprinklers throughout the entire building, or only within the altered area?

**Ideal answer:** The answer depends on NYC's alteration-trigger provisions rather than on the informal description "major alteration." BC §901.9 contains requirements for fire-protection systems in existing buildings and uses conditions such as the value and extent of alterations, occupancy, number of dwelling units, existing fire-protection systems, and other specific triggers. Some thresholds can require the altered portion to comply; other provisions can require broader work, particularly in buildings containing multiple dwelling units or where a separate retroactive requirement applies. Permitext must establish the building's occupancy, dwelling-unit count, valuation or extent of alteration under the applicable NYC method, existing sprinkler status, whether there is a change of use/occupancy or enlargement, and any independently applicable retroactive law. It should not infer either a whole-building or work-area-only sprinkler mandate from scope description alone.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §901.9 — Fire-protection requirements affecting existing buildings and alterations.
- Applicable §901.9 subsections for buildings containing four or more dwelling units.
- NYC Administrative Code §28-101.4.3 where prior-code building alteration provisions apply.
- Any separate retroactive sprinkler provision applicable to the building.

**Important qualifications:** Building occupancy, dwelling-unit count, alteration valuation/extent, existing systems, change of use, enlargement, and filing/code status are essential. For a 2026 filing, Permitext should not silently apply the future-effective NYC Existing Building Code.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Any major renovation requires sprinklers throughout the whole building.
- Sprinkler work is always limited to the work area.
- The future-effective Existing Building Code governs a 2026 alteration by default.
- Existing sprinkler conditions or retroactive laws are irrelevant.

## Test 09

**Q:** At what point does a proposed building require a standpipe system, and how should Permitext determine what type of standpipe is required?

**Ideal answer:** Permitext should begin with BC §905 rather than using a single story-count rule. The code identifies buildings and conditions requiring standpipes based on factors such as floor elevation relative to fire-department access, building height, occupancy, floor area, stages, underground conditions, and other special situations. Once a standpipe is required, the applicable class, system type, hose-connection locations, water supply and related requirements must be determined from §905 and the referenced NFPA standard as modified for New York City. A building can therefore require a standpipe without being a high-rise, and high-rise status can impose additional fire-protection requirements. Permitext should state the exact standpipe trigger and then separately establish the design requirements.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §905 — Standpipe systems.
- NYC BC §905.3 — Required installations.
- NYC BC §905.4 — Location of hose connections.
- NYC BC Appendix Q — NYC modifications to referenced fire-protection standards.

**Important qualifications:** Building height, highest and lowest floor elevations relative to fire-department access, occupancy, floor area, sprinkler status, and new-versus-existing condition are required facts.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Standpipes are required only after a certain number of stories.
- Every required standpipe uses the same class and system type.
- Appendix Q modifications can be ignored.
- A standpipe requirement is the same thing as a sprinkler requirement.

## Test 10

**Q:** Which systems in a high-rise residential building must be connected to emergency or legally required standby power?

**Ideal answer:** There is no reliable one-line list that can be generated from the word "high-rise" alone. BC §2702 provides the general emergency and standby power framework, while Chapter 4 high-rise provisions and individual fire-protection, elevator, smoke-control, egress-illumination, fire-pump, communications and mechanical provisions identify the systems that require backup power and whether emergency or standby power is required. Permitext should build the answer system-by-system and cite each trigger. It should distinguish emergency power from legally required standby power because transfer time, source, loads and system design can differ. A useful result would identify each applicable system, the section requiring backup power, the category of power required, and any duration or redundancy requirement in the approved evidence.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §2702 — Emergency and standby power systems.
- NYC BC §403 — High-rise buildings.
- Applicable NYC BC Chapter 9 provisions for fire pumps, fire alarm and smoke control.
- Applicable NYC BC Chapter 30 provisions for elevators.
- Current NYC Electrical Code provisions governing emergency and standby installations where electrical design is evaluated.

**Important qualifications:** Occupancy, actual high-rise condition, elevator arrangement, smoke-control design, fire pumps, communications systems, generator configuration and other building systems must be known. The electrical-code edition also must match the filing date.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- An uncited generic list of high-rise generator loads is sufficient.
- Emergency power and legally required standby power are the same category.
- Every electrical load in a high-rise must be backed up.
- An obsolete Electrical Code can be used for a current project without an applicability basis.

## Test 11

**Q:** A five-story residential building has no elevator. Does the number of stories alone establish that an elevator is required?

**Ideal answer:** No. Elevator requirements must be separated into accessibility scoping, building-use requirements, and special high-rise or occupancy provisions. Chapter 11 determines when an accessible route between stories is required and contains exceptions for certain multilevel buildings; Chapter 30 governs elevators once they are provided or required. A five-story building may have an accessibility obligation that effectively requires an elevator, but the conclusion depends on occupancy, number and location of dwelling units or common spaces, whether the project is new construction or an alteration, and the specific Chapter 11 exceptions. Story count by itself is not enough. Permitext should identify the scoping provision that requires vertical accessibility and then determine which elevator or lift type is permitted to satisfy it.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §1104 — Accessible routes.
- NYC BC §1104.4 — Multilevel buildings and facilities, including applicable exceptions.
- NYC BC Chapter 30 — Elevators and conveying systems.
- ICC A117.1 provisions incorporated by Chapter 11 for accessible elevators and lifts.

**Important qualifications:** New construction versus alteration is critical. Residential unit type, common-use spaces, elevator service area, and applicable prior-code-building provisions can change the result.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Every five-story building requires an elevator solely because it has five stories.
- Chapter 30 alone determines when vertical accessibility is required.
- A platform lift can always substitute for an elevator.
- Alteration-specific accessibility provisions are irrelevant.

## Test 12

**Q:** Do elevator doors opening directly onto a floor require an enclosed elevator lobby on every story?

**Ideal answer:** Not automatically. Elevator hoistway opening protection is governed by BC Chapter 30, including §3006, and the required protection depends on building height, occupancy, sprinkler status, hoistway configuration, number of stories connected, and the protection method permitted by the applicable subsection. An enclosed lobby is one possible method in some conditions, but the code can recognize other approved opening-protection strategies. High-rise provisions can impose additional elevator-lobby or smoke-protection requirements. Permitext should therefore identify the specific trigger for hoistway opening protection, determine whether an exception applies, and then evaluate the proposed method rather than assume every elevator door requires a lobby.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §3006 — Elevator lobbies and hoistway opening protection.
- NYC BC §403 — High-rise building provisions where applicable.
- NYC BC Chapter 7 provisions referenced by §3006 for smoke/fire separation.

**Important qualifications:** Number of stories connected, building height, occupancy, sprinkler status, elevator type, and whether the floor is at the level of exit discharge are relevant.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Every elevator requires an enclosed lobby on every floor.
- Sprinklers automatically eliminate hoistway opening protection.
- Smoke curtains, lobbies and rated doors are automatically equivalent solutions.
- High-rise-specific elevator provisions can be ignored.

## Test 13

**Q:** What structural live load should be used for a residential community room used for meetings, parties, and classes?

**Ideal answer:** Permitext should classify the room by its actual structural use and select the corresponding live load from BC Table 1607.1. The fact that the room is located in a residential building does not mean the dwelling-unit live load automatically applies. A community room used for meetings, parties, and classes has an assembly-type function, and the applicable assembly/public-room category should be evaluated based on whether seating is fixed, movable, standing use is anticipated, and what activities are permitted. If the room is genuinely multipurpose, the structural design should account for the most demanding applicable use unless a code-supported restriction limits the use. The exact Table 1607.1 row, not the building's overall R-2 classification, controls the design live load.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §1607 — Live loads.
- NYC BC Table 1607.1 — Minimum uniformly distributed and concentrated live loads.
- Any applicable occupancy/use restriction used to limit the room's structural design condition.

**Important qualifications:** Furniture layout, fixed versus movable seating, standing events, storage, stage areas, exercise activities and any posted/use restrictions can change the controlling load.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- The dwelling-unit live load applies because the room is in an R-2 building.
- Chapter 10 occupant-load factors are structural live loads.
- Typical attendance determines structural live load.
- The lowest plausible table category should be used for a multipurpose room.

## Test 14

**Q:** An existing office floor is being converted to dense file storage without changing columns or beams. Does the project require structural evaluation even though no structural construction is proposed?

**Ideal answer:** Yes, because the change in use can increase the imposed live load even if no structural member is physically altered. BC Chapter 16 requires floors and supporting members to resist applicable loads, and Table 1607.1 distinguishes office use from storage and file-related loading conditions. Permitext should compare the lawful or demonstrated capacity of the existing floor system with the load required for the proposed storage use, including concentrated loads where applicable. For a prior-code building, the special structural provisions governing existing buildings and alterations must also be considered. The absence of structural work does not establish that the existing structure is adequate for the proposed higher load.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §1601.2 — Special provisions for prior-code buildings where applicable.
- NYC BC §1607 and Table 1607.1 — Live loads.
- NYC Administrative Code §28-101.4.3 where prior-code alteration provisions apply.

**Important qualifications:** Existing structural drawings, original design loads, field conditions, storage layout, shelving system and concentrated loads are needed. A structural engineer may need to establish capacity from more than the code table alone.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- No structural review is needed because no beams are being modified.
- Office live load can be used for dense file storage.
- Existing capacity can be assumed from building age or construction material.
- Concentrated loads from shelving systems are irrelevant.

## Test 15

**Q:** A roof terrace guard is 42 inches high and uses horizontal rails. Does it comply with the NYC Building Code?

**Ideal answer:** The 42-inch height addresses only one part of the guard requirements. BC §1015 governs where guards are required, minimum height, opening limitations, geometry and special conditions; BC §1607.8 addresses structural loading on guards and handrails. Permitext must verify the actual guard height from the prescribed reference surface, allowable opening dimensions, special conditions at stairs or elevated surfaces, structural load resistance, and any NYC-specific provision affecting climbability or particular occupancies. Horizontal rails should not be declared acceptable or prohibited from intuition alone; the enacted §1015 text applicable to the occupancy and location must be checked. The defensible conclusion is that 42 inches can satisfy the general height criterion, but full guard compliance is not established by height and rail orientation alone.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §1015 — Guards.
- NYC BC §1607.8 — Loads on handrails and guards.
- Any applicable NYC-specific guard-opening or climbability subsection.

**Important qualifications:** Roof use, occupancy, fall height, measurement point, opening dimensions, attachment capacity and any special occupancy conditions are material.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- A 42-inch guard automatically complies.
- Horizontal rails are automatically prohibited without a supporting NYC provision.
- Guard opening limitations can be ignored.
- Structural design loads are irrelevant once the geometry complies.

## Test 16

**Q:** Does a typical interior exit stair require handrails on both sides, and may one handrail stop at an intermediate landing?

**Ideal answer:** BC §1011 requires stairways to have handrails in accordance with §1014, and the ordinary condition is handrails on both sides unless a specific exception applies. Handrail continuity is a separate requirement: the gripping surface generally must be continuous along the required flight and must extend, return or transition in the manner prescribed at landings and terminations. Whether one handrail can stop at an intermediate landing therefore depends on the actual stair configuration and the §1014 continuity/extension rules; the existence of a landing is not by itself permission to discontinue a required handrail. Permitext should also verify height, clearance, graspability, extensions and any intermediate handrail requirements.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §1011.11 — Stairway handrails.
- NYC BC §1014 — Handrails.
- Applicable §1014 subsections for continuity, height, extensions, clearance and graspability.

**Important qualifications:** Stair width, occupancy, switchback configuration, intermediate handrails, accessible design and dwelling-unit exceptions can change the result.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- One handrail is always enough.
- An intermediate landing automatically permits a required handrail to terminate.
- Handrail height is the only applicable requirement.
- Dwelling-unit or other specific exceptions apply to every stair.

## Test 17

**Q:** If a roof is designed for occupants as a terrace, does the occupied roof automatically count as another story above grade plane?

**Ideal answer:** No. An occupied roof and a story above grade plane are separate code concepts. The definition of story in BC §202 and the height-and-area provisions in Chapter 5 determine whether a level is a story, while occupied roofs are regulated through specific occupancy, egress, accessibility, structural and fire-protection provisions. An open occupied roof without an enclosing story generally does not become an additional story solely because people use it. Rooftop structures, penthouses and enclosed rooms must be analyzed separately, and Chapter 5 can cause rooftop structures to affect building height or story treatment when their aggregate area or configuration exceeds the permitted exception. Permitext should therefore answer both questions separately: whether the roof is occupiable and what regulations follow, and whether any rooftop enclosure creates an additional story.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §202 — Definitions of STORY and STORY ABOVE GRADE PLANE.
- NYC BC Chapter 5 — Building height and number of stories.
- NYC BC §504.3 — Rooftop structures excluded from height/story treatment under stated conditions.
- NYC BC §1510 — Rooftop structures.

**Important qualifications:** Enclosed rooftop rooms, bulkheads, penthouses, equipment enclosures and aggregate rooftop-structure area must be identified. Egress and accessibility can apply even when the roof is not a story.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Every occupied roof is automatically another story.
- An occupied roof has no egress/accessibility requirements because it is not a story.
- Rooftop enclosures can be ignored.
- Zoning treatment controls the Building Code story determination.

## Test 18

**Q:** An interior residential bathroom has no exterior window. Does it require mechanical exhaust, and where may that exhaust terminate?

**Ideal answer:** An interior bathroom cannot use natural ventilation through an exterior opening it does not have, so the applicable Building Code and Mechanical Code provisions must be checked for mechanical exhaust. BC Chapter 12 regulates ventilation of bathrooms and toilet rooms, while MC Chapters 4 and 5 regulate mechanical ventilation, exhaust-system construction and discharge. Permitext should determine the required exhaust rate from the applicable table or section and verify that the exhaust discharges to an approved outdoor location with the required separations from openings, property lines, outdoor-air intakes and other prohibited locations. Exhaust should not be assumed acceptable if it terminates in a ceiling plenum, attic, shaft, corridor or another room.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC BC §1203.5 — Ventilation of bathrooms and toilet rooms.
- NYC Mechanical Code §403 and Table 403.3.1.1 — Mechanical ventilation rates.
- NYC Mechanical Code Chapter 5 — Exhaust systems and discharge locations.

**Important qualifications:** Bathroom versus toilet-room classification, residential building type, intermittent versus continuous exhaust, duct route and termination location must be established.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- An interior bathroom can rely on transfer air without required exhaust.
- Exhaust can terminate in any shaft or ceiling cavity.
- A generic CFM value applies without the controlling table.
- Exhaust-outlet separation requirements are optional.

## Test 19

**Q:** A gas-fired appliance is located in a small mechanical room. Can the appliance obtain all required combustion air from the room itself?

**Ideal answer:** Only if the room and any qualifying communicating spaces satisfy the combustion-air method permitted by the applicable Mechanical and Fuel Gas Code provisions. Permitext must identify the appliance type, total input rating, room volume, building air-tightness, communicating-space conditions and whether the indoor-air method is permitted for that installation. If the room is too small or the building is too tight, combustion air must be provided through an approved outdoor-air, mechanical, combined or other code-permitted method. A louvered door or the statement that the room is "large enough" should not substitute for the required code calculation.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC Mechanical Code Chapter 7 — Combustion, ventilation and dilution air.
- Applicable NYC Fuel Gas Code combustion-air provisions for gas-fired appliances.
- Manufacturer installation instructions where the code makes them applicable.

**Important qualifications:** Appliance input ratings, room/communicating-space volume, exhaust equipment, air leakage, louvers/openings and appliance category are required facts.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- A mechanical room automatically supplies enough combustion air.
- Room floor area alone proves adequate combustion air.
- Exhaust systems that can depressurize the room are irrelevant.
- A louver of unspecified size solves the problem automatically.

## Test 20

**Q:** A mixed-use floor contains a restaurant, retail space, and office area. How should the required number of water closets and lavatories be calculated?

**Ideal answer:** Permitext should calculate plumbing fixtures by occupancy/use rather than apply one fixture ratio to the entire floor. PC §403.1 and Table 403.1 establish minimum fixture quantities by occupancy classification and description, using occupant loads determined under the Building Code. The restaurant, mercantile area and business office can therefore have different fixture ratios. The design occupant load for each use must be established first, then required water closets and lavatories calculated with the table's notes, rounding rules, current NYC toilet-room configuration provisions, employee/public requirements and any expressly permitted shared-facility arrangements. Fixtures should only be combined across tenants or uses where the Plumbing Code permits them to serve those occupants.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC Plumbing Code §403.1 — Minimum number of fixtures.
- NYC Plumbing Code Table 403.1 — Minimum number of required plumbing fixtures.
- NYC Plumbing Code §§403.2 through 403.6 as applicable to toilet-room configuration, access and facilities.
- NYC BC §1004 — Occupant load.

**Important qualifications:** Occupant load by use, separate-tenant status, employee/public access, shared-facility travel and operating arrangements are material facts.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- One fixture ratio applies to the entire mixed-use floor.
- Actual employee counts replace Building Code occupant loads where the table uses code occupant load.
- Separate tenants can always pool fixtures.
- Table 403.1 notes and rounding rules can be ignored.

## Test 21

**Q:** Can a single-occupant, all-gender toilet room be counted toward the required plumbing fixture total for occupants of any sex?

**Ideal answer:** Potentially yes, but Permitext must apply the current NYC Plumbing Code configuration rules rather than older assumptions about separate male and female rooms. The 2022 Plumbing Code includes NYC-specific provisions for single-occupant and multiple-occupant toilet rooms, and required or additional fixtures can be available for use by persons of any sex where the applicable sections permit. The fixture still has to satisfy the numerical requirements of Table 403.1, accessibility requirements where applicable, required lavatory location, signage and access provisions. A single-user room does not create extra fixture capacity beyond the fixtures actually installed; it changes who may use those fixtures and how they may be counted under the current code.

**Expected citations:**
- NYC Plumbing Code §403.1 and Table 403.1.
- NYC Plumbing Code §403.1.2 — Additional fixtures.
- NYC Plumbing Code §403.2 — Multiple-occupant toilet rooms.
- NYC Plumbing Code §403.4 — Signage.
- NYC Plumbing Code §405.3.2 — Public lavatories where applicable.

## Test 24

**Q:** A proposed exterior wall has enough cavity insulation that the total nominal R-values appear to exceed the code target, but it has less continuous insulation than the prescriptive table calls for. ## Test 30

**Q:** The code requires a smoke separation around a space. Does that automatically mean a 1-hour smoke barrier is required?

**Ideal answer:** No. A smoke barrier and a smoke partition are distinct assemblies with different construction, continuity, opening, leakage and rating requirements. BC §709 regulates smoke barriers and generally assigns fire-resistance requirements unless another section modifies them; BC §710 regulates smoke partitions and does not automatically impose the same hourly rating. The scoping provision for the specific occupancy or space determines which assembly is required. Permitext should trace the requirement back to the section that calls for the separation, identify whether it expressly requires a smoke barrier, smoke partition or another assembly, and then apply the corresponding Chapter 7 requirements. The generic phrase "smoke separation" is not sufficient to infer a 1-hour wall.

**Expected citations:**

- NYC BC §710 — Smoke partitions.
- The occupancy or special-use provision that requires the smoke separation.
- NYC BC §§716 and 717 where openings or ducts in the assembly are regulated.

**Important qualifications:** The exact scoping language is outcome-determinative. Special occupancies can modify rating, continuity, door, leakage or damper requirements.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- Smoke barriers and smoke partitions are interchangeable terms.
- Every smoke separation is 1-hour rated.
- A drawing note controls over the governing code section.
- Openings and ducts can be ignored once the wall type is identified.

## Test 35

**Q:** An intermediate floor level occupies approximately one-third of the room below and is open to that room. Does it qualify as a mezzanine rather than an additional story?

**Ideal answer:** Potentially, but area alone does not establish mezzanine status. - NYC BC §505.2 — Mezzanine area limitation.

- Other mezzanines in the same space can be ignored.
- A small enclosed independent floor is a mezzanine solely because of its size.

## Test 36

**Q:** A platform supports only mechanical equipment and is accessed by maintenance staff. Is it a mezzanine, an equipment platform, or another story?

- Aggregate area of multiple equipment platforms can be ignored.

## Test 38

**Q:** Can the required fire-resistance rating of the building's structural frame be reduced by one hour simply because the building is fully sprinklered?

## Test 39

**Q:** The Building Code appears to permit a condition, but an applicable DOB rule imposes an additional requirement. Which one should Permitext use?

**Ideal answer:** Permitext should not automatically discard either source. The Building Code, Administrative Code, duly promulgated DOB rules, Local Laws and other incorporated authorities can regulate the same condition at different levels of specificity. A DOB rule can implement or supplement a code requirement within delegated authority; it is not automatically invalid simply because the Building Code is less specific. Permitext should cite both sources, establish that the rule is current and applicable, identify its legal relationship to the code, and apply the additional requirement where the rule validly supplements the code. If the texts genuinely conflict rather than supplement one another, Permitext should flag the conflict and the need for an official interpretation or determination rather than silently choose whichever result seems stricter.

**Expected citations:**
- The specific NYC Building Code provision governing the condition.
- The specific current 1 RCNY/DOB rule governing the same condition.
- Applicable NYC Administrative Code rulemaking/authority provisions if hierarchy must be resolved.

**Important qualifications:** Effective date, project type, scope, delegated authority, and whether the secondary source is a promulgated rule, bulletin, interpretation or guidance document must be distinguished.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- The stricter requirement always wins without analyzing legal authority.
- A current DOB rule can be ignored whenever the Building Code is silent.
- A guidance document has the same legal status as a promulgated rule without further analysis.
- Permitext can invent a hierarchy when the authorities genuinely conflict.

## Test 40

**Q:** A Local Law changes a Building Code requirement after a permit application was filed but before construction begins. Which version applies?

**Ideal answer:** Permitext should treat this as an effective-date and transition question rather than assume that either filing date or construction date always controls. The enacted Local Law normally contains an effective-date clause and can specify whether it applies to applications filed on or after that date, permits issued after that date, work commenced after that date, amendments, or another transition condition. The Administrative Code and DOB transition provisions can also preserve an earlier code for qualifying applications while requiring certain later amendments or safety provisions to follow current law. Permitext should retrieve the enacted Local Law, read its effective-date/applicability clause, establish the application's filing date, completeness, approval/permit status and scope, and check any official DOB transition guidance. Only then can it determine which text governs.

**Expected citations:**
- The enacted Local Law, including its effective-date and applicability clause.
- NYC Administrative Code §28-101 and the applicable code-transition provisions.
- DOB Buildings Bulletin, Service Notice or other official transition guidance issued for the amendment, if any.
- The affected code section before and after amendment where comparison is necessary.

**Important qualifications:** Filing date alone may not be sufficient. Application completeness, permit issuance, amendments, scope changes, specific statutory transition wording and any special retroactive provision can control.

**Claims Permitext must avoid:**
- The newest code always applies because construction has not started.
- Filing an application always locks every requirement to the older code.
- The effective date can be applied without reading the Local Law's transition clause.
- A proposed bill or pending amendment can be treated as enacted law.
