APPENDIX A

STRENGTHENING PROVISIONS FOR UNREINFORCED MASONRY WALLS,

Enacted, not yet effective. Effective July 17, 2027.

EBC A101 SCOPE

A101.1 General. This Appendix gives requirements for connecting walls to floors and parapets
and chimneys to roofs for existing unreinforced masonry buildings with wood framed floors or
roofs, where required by Chapter 7.
The provisions of this Appendix are intended as minimum standards for incrementally improving
structural seismic resistance and are established primarily to reduce the risk of loss, damage to
property, or injury to persons. Compliance with these provisions will not necessarily prevent loss
of life or injury or prevent earthquake damage to retrofitted buildings.
This Appendix is based on a seismic design criteria limit of Sd1<=.133, and wind conditions of
Exposure D. This Appendix does not apply to essential or hazardous facilities.
A101.2 Definitions.
UNREINFORCED MASONRY (URM). Includes burned clay, concrete, or sand-lime brick;
hollow clay or concrete block; plain concrete; and hollow clay tile.
UNREINFORCED MASONRY WALL. A masonry wall that:
1. Relies solely on the tensile strength of masonry units, mortar, and grout in resisting design
loads. Any area of reinforcement that may be present is neglected in determining resistance to
loads; and
2. Carries a minimum service load of 100 pounds per lineal foot (1459 N/m) of roof and/or
floor loads.

EBC A102 MASONRY WALL CONNECTIONS

A102.1 Connection locations. Unreinforced masonry walls shall be connected at the roof and
floor levels.
A102.1.1 Connection requirements. Connections shall consist of existing connections and/or
new connections. New connections shall be as shown in Figures A1-1, A1-2, or A1-3 where
applicable, and provided at a maximum plan spacing of 5 feet and 4 inches (1625 mm) on
center. A registered design professional can provide alternative anchor spacings and details
for loads conforming to the New York City Building Code. All wall connections shall be
secured to the framing members parallel or perpendicular to the wall. Cross-grain bending in
wood members used for wall connections is prohibited.
A102.2 Minimum wall connection capacity. Connection of walls to each floor or roof shall be
able to resist a minimum service load of 200 pounds per linear foot (2919 N/m), acting normal to
the wall at the level of the floor or roof. Existing wall connections, if used, must meet the
requirements of this chapter, or must be supplemented with new connections to comply with
requirements.
A102.2.1 Existing wall connections capacity. Existing connections used as all or part of the
required wall connections shall be considered to have a service tension capacity of 100 pounds
(445 N), unless tested or evaluated by a registered design professional. Friction shall be
neglected in determining existing connection capacity.
A102.2.2 Wall connections per Chapter 33 of the New York City Building Code. Wall
connections installed as required by Section 3309 and attached to joists in a manner similar to
Figure A1-1 may be deemed to comply with the requirements of this Appendix.
A102.3 Wall connection testing and inspection. New wall connections shall be tested and
inspected in accordance with the requirements of Chapter 17 of the New York City Building Code,
and as required by the connection manufacturer. Testing of existing wall connections, if required,
shall be specified and evaluated by a registered design professional.
A102.4 Existing wall wythe bonding and pattern. A registered design professional shall confirm
that the existing exterior wall wythes that are being anchored are properly bonded together. If
wythes are not properly bonded together, or the bonding pattern of wythes is different from header
courses every sixth course, a registered design professional shall evaluate the conditions and
design repairs if required.

EBC A103 PARAPETS AND CHIMNEYS

A103.1 Parapet and Chimney heights. The maximum height of an unbraced unreinforced
masonry parapet or chimney above the lower of either the level of anchors or the roof sheathing
shall not exceed 3.0 times its thickness. Other ratios may be used if justified by a registered design
professional. Parapets and chimneys not conforming to this chapter shall be removed, reduced in
height if allowed, stabilized, strengthened, or laterally braced to ensure that the parapets and
chimneys remain in their original positions and conform to this chapter.
A103.2 Lateral bracing. If the parapet or chimney height exceeds the allowed maximum, and
new lateral bracing is chosen as a remedy, then the lateral bracing system shall be as shown in
Figures A1-4 through A1-6 as applicable. A registered design professional can provide alternative
bracing details for loads conforming to the New York City Building Code. Parapet and chimney
corrective work is to be performed in conjunction with the installation of roof connections.
A103.3 Existing coping connections. The stability and strength of connections of parapet and
chimney copings shall be evaluated by a registered design professional.
