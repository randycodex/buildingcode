# Permitext NYC Zoning Research Evaluation Cases — Batch 1

The following batch contains **12 independently drafted evaluation cases**: seven are **READY** for Permitext owner review, and five are **BLOCKED FOR SOURCE VERIFICATION**. “READY” means the official evidence is sufficiently verified to define the expected test response; it does **not** mean the hypothetical project is approved or legally compliant.

The baseline used is the **current official online NYC Zoning Resolution as accessed August 30, 2026**. Where relevant, Appendix F is dated August 13, 2026, Appendix B is dated November 12, 2025, and the City of Yes for Housing Opportunity text amendment, N 240290 ZRY, was adopted December 5, 2024. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/appendix-f-mandatory-inclusionary-housing-areas-and-former-inclusionary-housing-designated-areas))

## Case 1

**DRAFT FOR PERMITEXT OWNER REVIEW**

**Case title:** R8A mixed-use development relying on an unverified affordable-housing FAR
**Category:** FAR / mixed use / affordable housing / mapped conditions / special districts

**Realistic customer question:**

I have a 12,000-square-foot corner lot that the broker says is in an R8A district. We want apartments over ground-floor retail, with 85,000 square feet of total zoning floor area. The broker also says the site is in a transit zone and might be in an MIH area. Can we build that amount?

**Known facts:**
- Claimed zoning district: R8A.
- Claimed zoning lot area: 12,000 square feet.
- The lot is described as a corner lot.
- Proposed building contains residences and ground-floor retail.
- Proposed total zoning floor area: 85,000 square feet.
- The property is claimed to be in a transit zone.
- MIH status is uncertain.
- Special-district status is unknown.

**Missing or uncertain facts:**
- Property address, borough, block and lot.
- Verified zoning-lot boundaries and zoning-lot area.
- Official zoning map and exact district boundaries.
- Whether a C1 or C2 commercial overlay or a Commercial District is mapped on the lot.
- Exact proposed retail use and its use classification.
- Residential, commercial and shared-floor-area breakdown.
- Whether the property is in an MIH area under Appendix F.
- Which MIH options apply, if any.
- Whether the proposal qualifies as a UAP development outside an MIH area.
- Amount of legally qualifying affordable floor area.
- HPD application, restrictive declaration, regulatory agreement and permit-notice status.
- Whether a Special Purpose District modifies use, FAR, height or affordable-housing rules.
- Whether any district boundary crosses the zoning lot.
- Whether any floor-area deductions, bonuses or transfers are proposed.
- Whether the site is in the Inner Transit Zone, Outer Transit Zone, another special parking area, or merely near transit.

**Applicable date or code edition:**

Current official online NYC Zoning Resolution as accessed August 30, 2026. Appendix F dated August 13, 2026 must be used for current MIH mapping. No historical or transition claim has been provided.

**Official evidence:**

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §23-22, Maximum Floor Area Ratio for R6–R12 Districts
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-22
  **What it establishes:** R8A permits a maximum residential FAR of 6.02 for standard residences and 7.20 for zoning lots containing qualifying affordable housing or qualifying senior housing. The two values represent different legal categories, not an elective range. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-22))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §35-31, Maximum Floor Area Ratio
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-iii/chapter-5/35-31
  **What it establishes:** Where Chapter 35 applies to a mixed building in a Commercial District, the individual residential and non-residential FAR limits apply, but their total generally may not exceed the greatest FAR permitted for any applicable use unless another provision expressly says otherwise. Residential and commercial FARs are not automatically added together. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-iii/chapter-5/35-31))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §§22-00, 22-10 and 32-10
  **Official URLs:**
  https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-2/22-00
  https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-2/22-10
  https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-iii/chapter-2/32-10
  **What they establish:** Use permission depends on the identified use, applicable Use Group table and mapped district. Special-area provisions may modify the underlying use rules. The phrase “ground-floor retail” is not sufficiently precise to determine permission. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-2/22-00))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §12-10 and ZR §27-111, definitions of qualifying affordable housing, UAP development and transit zones
  **Official URLs:**
  https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-i/chapter-2/12-10
  https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-7/27-111
  **What they establish:** A UAP development is an eligible development or enlargement outside an MIH area that provides qualifying affordable housing. Its residential FAR may exceed the standard-residence FAR only by the amount of qualifying affordable housing provided. Transit-zone status is a separate mapped concept and does not itself establish MIH or UAP qualification. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-7/27-111))

- **ZR section/table/map:** Appendix F; Appendix B; official ZoLa mapping
  **Official URLs:**
  https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/appendix-f-mandatory-inclusionary-housing-areas-and-former-inclusionary-housing-designated-areas
  https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/appendix-b-index-special-purpose-districts
  https://zola.planning.nyc.gov/
  **What they establish:** MIH and Special Purpose District applicability are property-specific mapped conditions. Appendix F identifies MIH areas through incorporated maps; Appendix B indexes special districts and their zoning maps. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/appendix-f-mandatory-inclusionary-housing-areas-and-former-inclusionary-housing-designated-areas))

**Expected safe answer:**

The proposal cannot be determined compliant from the supplied facts. Its proposed overall FAR is approximately **7.083**, but that number cannot be compared indiscriminately with the R8A qualifying-affordable-housing FAR of 7.20.

For an illustrative residential-only comparison, 12,000 square feet of lot area would permit 72,240 square feet at the standard R8A FAR of 6.02 or 86,400 square feet at the qualifying FAR of 7.20. The proposed 85,000 square feet would therefore exceed the standard numerical amount by 12,760 square feet and fall 1,400 square feet below the qualifying numerical ceiling. That comparison does not establish legal qualification because the proposal is mixed-use, the residential and retail floor areas are not separated, the commercial mapping is unverified, and neither MIH nor UAP qualification has been established. Transit-zone status alone does not provide the qualifying FAR. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-22))

**Required concepts:**
- Overall proposed FAR versus use-specific FAR.
- Standard-residence FAR versus qualifying-affordable-housing FAR.
- Numerical fit versus legal qualification.
- MIH mapping versus UAP qualification outside MIH.
- Transit-zone mapping as a separate issue.
- Commercial-use permission and mapped commercial overlay.
- Mixed-building total FAR rule.
- Special-district and district-boundary review.
- Exact zoning-lot boundaries.

**Required calculation, if any:**
- Proposed overall FAR:
  85,000 sf ÷ 12,000 sf = **7.083 FAR**
- Standard R8A residential maximum:
  12,000 sf × 6.02 = **72,240 sf**
- Amount above standard maximum:
  85,000 sf − 72,240 sf = **12,760 sf**
- Qualifying R8A table maximum:
  12,000 sf × 7.20 = **86,400 sf**
- Amount below qualifying table maximum:
  86,400 sf − 85,000 sf = **1,400 sf**
- Assumption warning: these comparisons treat the entire 85,000 square feet as though it were residential solely to illustrate the numerical issue. The actual mixed-use calculation requires the floor-area breakdown.

**Forbidden or unsafe claims:**
- “Yes, because 7.083 is less than 7.20.”
- “Being in a transit zone makes the project eligible for the affordable-housing FAR.”
- “R8A automatically permits ground-floor retail.”
- “The residential and commercial FARs can be added together.”
- “The property is in MIH because the broker said it might be.”
- “The 85,000-square-foot proposal is as-of-right.”
- “The project is code-compliant” or “zoning-compliant.”

**Necessary follow-up questions:**
- What is the address and borough, block and lot?
- What are the recorded zoning-lot boundaries?
- What is the exact residential, commercial and shared-floor-area breakdown?
- What specific retail establishment is proposed?
- What commercial overlay or Commercial District is mapped?
- Is the zoning lot inside an Appendix F MIH boundary?
- Is it in a Special Purpose District?
- Is qualifying affordable housing proposed, and what is the verified affordable floor area?
- Has HPD issued or reviewed an affordable housing application, restrictive declaration, regulatory agreement or permit notice?
- Which transit-zone category applies?

**Evidence boundary:**

The selected text establishes the base R8A FAR categories and the general mixed-building rule. It cannot establish mapped commercial permission, MIH applicability, Special Purpose District applicability, exact transit-zone geography, affordable-housing qualification or the correct mixed-use FAR without the property location and project floor-area breakdown.

**Source-verification status:**

**BLOCKED FOR SOURCE VERIFICATION** — the official property-specific zoning map, Appendix F map, special-district map, transit-zone layer and zoning-lot records cannot be verified without an address or BBL.

**Why this is a valuable Research test:**

This tests whether Permitext resists a superficially easy “7.08 is below 7.20” answer and instead distinguishes a numerical ceiling from affordable-housing qualification, mapped use permission, mixed-use rules and property-specific overlays.

## Case 2

**DRAFT FOR PERMITEXT OWNER REVIEW**

**Case title:** R6A UAP proposal with insufficient affordable floor area
**Category:** UAP / affordable housing / FAR calculation

**Realistic customer question:**

My 8,000-square-foot R6A lot is outside an MIH area. I want 31,000 square feet of residential floor area, including 6,200 square feet that I intend to make affordable. Since 31,000 square feet is below the R6A 3.90 FAR limit, can I build it?

**Known facts:**
- Zoning district: R6A.
- Zoning-lot area: 8,000 square feet.
- The lot is outside an MIH area.
- No Special Purpose District or mapped FAR modification applies.
- Proposed residential floor area: 31,000 square feet.
- Claimed affordable floor area: 6,200 square feet.
- The proposal would be a post-December 5, 2024 development.
- No other floor-area bonus or transfer is proposed.

**Missing or uncertain facts:**
- Whether the claimed 6,200 square feet satisfies the ZR definition of affordable floor area.
- Affordable-unit floor areas and proportional eligible common areas.
- Proposed income bands and weighted-average income.
- Whether the project has an HPD affordable housing application.
- Whether an HPD-approved restrictive declaration or affordable housing regulatory agreement will be recorded.
- Whether HPD has issued a permit notice.
- Whether off-site affordable housing is proposed.
- Whether any of the stated floor areas include non-zoning floor area.

**Applicable date or code edition:**

Current official online NYC Zoning Resolution as accessed August 30, 2026, applying the UAP provisions established in connection with N 240290 ZRY after December 5, 2024.

**Official evidence:**

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §23-22
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-22
  **What it establishes:** R6A has a standard-residence FAR of 3.00 and a qualifying-affordable-housing or qualifying-senior-housing table maximum of 3.90. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-22))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §27-111, definitions of UAP development, UAP site and affordable floor area
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-7/27-111
  **What it establishes:** Outside MIH, a UAP development may exceed the standard-residence FAR only by the amount of qualifying affordable housing provided. Affordable floor area includes qualifying unit floor area and the applicable proportion of eligible common areas; it is not merely any area the applicant labels affordable. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-7/27-111))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §27-151
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-7/27-151
  **What it establishes:** HPD permit notices, recorded restrictive declarations and completion notices form part of the administrative requirements for UAP and MIH developments, subject to the section’s exceptions. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-7/27-151))

**Expected safe answer:**

No, not based on the stated 6,200 square feet of affordable floor area. Although the proposal’s FAR of 3.875 is below the R6A table maximum of 3.90 for qualifying affordable housing, a UAP project outside MIH may exceed the standard 3.00 FAR only by the amount of qualifying affordable housing provided.

The standard residential allowance is 24,000 square feet. Adding 6,200 square feet of verified affordable floor area produces a preliminary UAP maximum of 30,200 square feet, which is 800 square feet below the proposed 31,000 square feet. The project would need at least 7,000 square feet of qualifying affordable floor area to support the proposed increase above the standard amount, subject to the overall 3.90 table maximum and all HPD documentation requirements. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-22))

**Required concepts:**
- The 3.90 FAR is a ceiling for a legally qualifying category, not an automatic entitlement.
- UAP applies outside MIH.
- The UAP increment is limited by actual qualifying affordable floor area.
- Affordable floor area must be calculated under the ZR definition.
- HPD documentation and recording requirements.
- Table maximum versus project-specific UAP maximum.

**Required calculation, if any:**
- Proposed FAR:
  31,000 sf ÷ 8,000 sf = **3.875 FAR**
- Standard R6A allowance:
  8,000 sf × 3.00 = **24,000 sf**
- R6A qualifying table ceiling:
  8,000 sf × 3.90 = **31,200 sf**
- UAP amount supported by stated affordable floor area:
  24,000 sf + 6,200 sf = **30,200 sf**
- Excess over stated UAP-supported amount:
  31,000 sf − 30,200 sf = **800 sf**
- Affordable floor area needed to support 31,000 sf:
  31,000 sf − 24,000 sf = **7,000 sf**
- Additional qualifying affordable floor area needed:
  7,000 sf − 6,200 sf = **800 sf**

**Forbidden or unsafe claims:**
- “Any R6A development may use 3.90 FAR.”
- “The project qualifies because 3.875 is below 3.90.”
- “Six thousand two hundred square feet of apartments marketed as affordable automatically counts as affordable floor area.”
- “HPD approval is unnecessary.”
- “The proposal is approved if another 800 square feet is labeled affordable.”

**Necessary follow-up questions:**
- How was the 6,200-square-foot affordable-floor-area figure calculated?
- What unit and common-area floor areas are included?
- What affordability restrictions and income bands are proposed?
- Has an affordable housing application been submitted to HPD?
- Will an approved restrictive declaration or regulatory agreement be recorded?
- Has HPD issued a permit notice?
- Is any off-site UAP option involved?

**Evidence boundary:**

The selected provisions establish the preliminary FAR arithmetic and UAP qualification framework. They do not establish that the stated affordable area legally qualifies, that HPD will accept the proposed affordability structure, or that the building satisfies height, yard, unit-density, recreation-space or other zoning requirements.

**Source-verification status:**

**READY** — the official text is sufficient to establish the expected conditional answer and calculation. Project qualification remains deliberately unresolved.

**Why this is a valuable Research test:**

This tests whether Permitext recognizes that a qualifying-affordable-housing FAR is not fully available merely because some affordable units are proposed. It requires the system to apply the one-for-one UAP increment and identify the HPD evidence boundary.

## Case 3

**DRAFT FOR PERMITEXT OWNER REVIEW**

**Case title:** Market-rate R7A building incorrectly using the qualifying height limit
**Category:** Height and setback / affordable-housing distinction

**Realistic customer question:**

We are designing a completely market-rate residential building in an R7A district. The main roof would be 105 feet high. I saw that R7A buildings can be 115 feet, so is 105 feet permitted?

**Known facts:**
- Zoning district: R7A.
- Proposed use: residential.
- The building is entirely market-rate.
- It does not contain qualifying affordable housing or qualifying senior housing.
- Proposed main-roof height: 105 feet.
- No Special Purpose District or other mapped height modification applies.

**Missing or uncertain facts:**
- Proposed base height.
- Location and depth of the required setback.
- Street-wall location and width.
- Whether rooftop structures or permitted obstructions extend above the main roof.
- Whether the applicable height is measured from the correct base plane.
- Whether any separate transit-adjacent, flood-zone or airport limitation applies.

**Applicable date or code edition:**

Current official online NYC Zoning Resolution as accessed August 30, 2026.

**Official evidence:**

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §23-432, Height and Setback Requirements
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-432
  **What it establishes:** In R7A, the minimum base height is 40 feet. The standard-residence maximum base height is 75 feet and maximum building height is 85 feet. The qualifying-affordable-housing or qualifying-senior-housing maximum base height is 85 feet and maximum building height is 115 feet. A portion exceeding the maximum base height requires a setback under ZR §23-433. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-432))

**Expected safe answer:**

No. Based on the supplied facts, the building is subject to the **standard-residence maximum height of 85 feet**, not the 115-foot maximum reserved for zoning lots containing qualifying affordable housing or qualifying senior housing. A 105-foot main roof would exceed the applicable standard maximum by 20 feet.

Reducing the roof to 85 feet would resolve only the stated overall-height issue. The design would still need to satisfy the 40-foot minimum base height, 75-foot standard maximum base height, required setback and all other applicable bulk rules. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-432))

**Required concepts:**
- Separate height columns for standard and qualifying residences.
- Affordable-housing qualification as a legal condition.
- Maximum building height versus maximum base height.
- Setback requirement above the base.
- Overall height compliance does not establish complete bulk compliance.

**Required calculation, if any:**
- Proposed height: **105 ft**
- Applicable standard maximum: **85 ft**
- Excess:
  105 ft − 85 ft = **20 ft**
- The 115-foot value is not used because the known facts expressly state that the building is not qualifying affordable housing or qualifying senior housing.

**Forbidden or unsafe claims:**
- “R7A permits 115 feet for every residential building.”
- “The building qualifies because it is below 115 feet.”
- “The design is compliant if the roof is lowered to 85 feet.”
- “Market-rate units may use the affordable-housing height column.”

**Necessary follow-up questions:**
- What are the proposed base height and setback dimensions?
- What base plane is being used?
- Are any rooftop obstructions proposed?
- Is the site subject to flood, transit-adjacent, airport or other special-area height rules?

**Evidence boundary:**

The selected evidence determines the standard R7A base and building-height limits. It does not evaluate street walls, setback depth, permitted obstructions, lot conditions or other mapped controls.

**Source-verification status:**

**READY** — the known facts select the standard-residence column, and the height comparison is determinable from the current official table.

**Why this is a valuable Research test:**

This is a direct test of column selection. It catches answers that retrieve the largest number in a table without determining which legal category applies.

## Case 4

**DRAFT FOR PERMITEXT OWNER REVIEW**

**Case title:** Insufficient rear yard equivalent on a deep through lot
**Category:** Yards / through lots / vertical dimensional change

**Realistic customer question:**

We have a 200-foot-deep through lot in an R7A district. The design has residential wings facing both streets with a 30-foot-wide open area between them. Both wings rise to 100 feet. Is the 30-foot open area enough for the rear yard equivalent?

**Known facts:**
- Zoning district: R7A.
- The zoning lot is a through lot.
- Maximum street-to-street depth: 200 feet.
- Open area between the two building wings: 30 feet.
- Both wings rise to 100 feet.
- The lot is not a large site.
- The zoning lot does not occupy the entire block.
- None of the other stated exceptions in ZR §23-343 applies.
- No alternative-location provision is being used.

**Missing or uncertain facts:**
- Whether the open area is midway, or within 10 feet of midway, between the two street lines.
- Whether the open area is unobstructed from its lowest level to the sky except for permitted obstructions.
- Exact geometry along the entire depth and width of the building.
- Whether any portion of the lot qualifies as a shallow-lot condition existing on December 15, 1961.
- Whether a portion of the building changes height within the open-area zone.

**Applicable date or code edition:**

Current official online NYC Zoning Resolution as accessed August 30, 2026.

**Official evidence:**

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §12-10, definition of through lot
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-i/chapter-2/12-10
  **What it establishes:** A through lot is generally a zoning lot, other than a corner lot, adjoining two opposite street lines that are parallel or within 45 degrees of parallel. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-i/chapter-2/12-10))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §23-343, Rear Yard Equivalent Requirements
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-343
  **What it establishes:** On a through lot at least 190 feet deep, the rear yard equivalent must have a minimum depth of 40 feet for building portions at or below 75 feet and 60 feet above 75 feet. The standard location is midway, or within 10 feet of midway, between the street lines. The section also contains specified exceptions and alternative-location provisions. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-343))

**Expected safe answer:**

No. On the stated 200-foot-deep through lot, the 30-foot open area is insufficient at every relevant height. At and below 75 feet, the required rear yard equivalent is 40 feet, creating a 10-foot deficiency. Above 75 feet, the required depth is 60 feet, creating a 30-foot deficiency for the portions of the wings extending to 100 feet.

The design must also verify that the required open area is in the permitted location and remains unobstructed as required. Merely providing open space between two wings does not make that space a compliant rear yard equivalent. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-343))

**Required concepts:**
- Confirmation that the lot is a through lot.
- Deep-lot threshold of 190 feet.
- Separate requirement at or below 75 feet.
- Increased requirement above 75 feet.
- Midpoint-location requirement.
- Exceptions must be checked before applying the standard rule.
- Open area versus legally compliant rear yard equivalent.

**Required calculation, if any:**
- Lot depth: **200 ft**, which is 10 feet greater than the 190-foot standard-lot threshold.
- At or below 75 feet:
  40 ft required − 30 ft provided = **10 ft deficiency**
- Above 75 feet:
  60 ft required − 30 ft provided = **30 ft deficiency**
- Building height above the change point:
  100 ft − 75 ft = **25 ft of building height subject to the 60-foot requirement**

**Forbidden or unsafe claims:**
- “Thirty feet is a standard rear yard, so it is sufficient.”
- “The same rear-yard-equivalent depth applies at every height.”
- “Any open area between buildings qualifies.”
- “The open area complies without checking its location.”
- “The project must be rejected,” rather than identifying that the shown configuration does not comply with the selected rule.

**Necessary follow-up questions:**
- Is the open area located midway, or within 10 feet of midway, between the street lines?
- Is it unobstructed from its lowest level to the sky?
- Is any shallow-lot historical condition claimed?
- Do any building portions stop at or below 75 feet?
- Is an eligible alternative-location method proposed?

**Evidence boundary:**

The selected evidence determines the rear-yard-equivalent depth and standard location. It does not determine permitted obstructions, the exact surveyed lot geometry or compliance of a redesigned configuration.

**Source-verification status:**

**READY** — the supplied lot depth and building height are sufficient to apply the current dimensional rule, subject to the expressly identified location and obstruction questions.

**Why this is a valuable Research test:**

This tests whether Permitext detects a requirement that changes vertically at 75 feet instead of returning a single yard dimension for the entire building.

## Case 5

**DRAFT FOR PERMITEXT OWNER REVIEW**

**Case title:** R6 parking requirement based on an unverified transit-zone claim
**Category:** Parking / transit-zone maps / waivers / effective date

**Realistic customer question:**

We are planning a new 60-unit market-rate apartment building in an R6 district. It will be completed after December 5, 2024. The broker says it is close to a subway, so no parking should be required. Is that correct?

**Known facts:**
- Zoning district: R6.
- Proposed development: 60 standard dwelling units.
- Units are not qualifying senior housing, income-restricted units in qualifying affordable housing or ancillary dwelling units.
- Units will be created after December 5, 2024.
- The property is described as close to a subway.

**Missing or uncertain facts:**
- Property address and BBL.
- Whether the site is in the Inner Transit Zone.
- Whether it is in the Outer Transit Zone.
- Whether it is within another special parking area.
- Whether it is beyond the Greater Transit Zone.
- Whether the textual transit-zone boundary differs from the ZoLa display.
- Whether the site has any special-district parking modification.
- Whether the site physically cannot arrange compliant parking access under ZR §25-63.
- Exact temporary or final certificate-of-occupancy date for determining when the units are “created.”

**Applicable date or code edition:**

Current official online NYC Zoning Resolution as accessed August 30, 2026, using the post-December 5, 2024 parking geography and requirements.

**Official evidence:**

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §12-10, Greater, Inner and Outer Transit Zone definitions
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-i/chapter-2/12-10
  **What it establishes:** Transit-zone status is determined through defined boundaries identified in the Resolution and on the official ZoLa transit-zone layer. The Greater Transit Zone includes the Inner Transit Zone, Outer Transit Zone and special parking areas. If the mapped display conflicts with the textual boundary, the text controls. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-i/chapter-2/12-10))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §25-211
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-5/25-211
  **What it establishes:** In the Inner Transit Zone, no accessory off-street parking is required for dwelling units or rooming units created after December 5, 2024. “Created” is tied to issuance of the applicable temporary or final certificate of occupancy. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-5/25-211))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §25-222
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-5/25-222
  **What it establishes:** In the Outer Transit Zone, standard R6 dwelling units generally require parking at 25 percent, but the entire residential requirement is waived where the calculated number is 15 spaces or fewer. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-5/25-222))

- **ZR section/table/map:** ZR §25-232
  **Official URL:** https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-5/25-232
  **What it establishes:** Beyond the Greater Transit Zone, standard R6 dwelling units generally require parking at 50 percent, with a maximum waiver of 10 spaces. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-5/25-232))

**Expected safe answer:**

“Close to a subway” is not enough to determine the parking requirement. The answer changes materially with the official parking geography:

- In the **Inner Transit Zone**, the stated post-December 5, 2024 units would require **zero spaces**.
- In the **Outer Transit Zone**, 60 standard units at 25 percent produce 15 spaces; because 15 is at the R6 waiver threshold, **zero spaces** would be required under that table.
- **Beyond the Greater Transit Zone**, 60 standard units at 50 percent produce **30 spaces**. Because 30 exceeds the 10-space waiver threshold, the table would require 30 spaces unless another provision applies.
- A separately defined special parking area or special district may produce a different result.

The exact address and official mapped/textual transit-zone classification are therefore controlling. ([zr.planning.nyc.gov](https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-5/25-211))

**Required concepts:**
- “Near transit” versus legally mapped transit-zone status.
- Inner Transit Zone versus Outer Transit Zone.
